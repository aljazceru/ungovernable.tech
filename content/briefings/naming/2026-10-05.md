### 1. ICANN Reveal Day: October 7, 2026 at 18:00 UTC — full week earlier than expected

**Announcement**: [icann.org/en/announcements/details/icann-announces-date-for-reveal-day-and-other-2026-round-milestones-29-09-2026-en](https://www.icann.org/en/announcements/details/icann-announces-date-for-reveal-day-and-other-2026-round-milestones-29-09-2026-en) — **Sep 29 2026**.
**Coverage**: [circleid.com/posts/icann-sets-october-7-for-2026-new-gtld-reveal-day](https://circleid.com/posts/icann-sets-october-7-for-2026-new-gtld-reveal-day) — **Sep 30 2026**.

The Sep 29 ICANN announcement is the **institutional close of the 2026 Round submission phase**. It confirms:

- **Reveal Day: October 7, 2026 at 18:00 UTC** — published on the 2026 Round Application Publication and Statistics (APS) website. Disclosed: applicant identities, all primary strings, applicable variant and replacement strings, contention sets, public portions of each application.
- **Replacement Period: October 8 2026 00:01 UTC – October 21 2026 23:59 UTC** — 2-week window. Eligible applicants that submitted an alternate replacement string may switch from their primary string. **A replacement cannot be used if it is identical to another primary or replacement string**, although a replacement could still enter contention later (e.g., singular/plural notifications, String Confusion Objections).
- **String Confirmation Day: November 17, 2026** — final list of all applied-for gTLD strings + updated contention sets. **Starts the Community Input and Objections Period** + **opens the 10-day withdrawal window for a 65% refund** of the $227,000 evaluation fee.
- **ICANN office closure 1:00 UTC 19 December 2026 – 16:00 UTC 4 January 2027** — pause factored into the Community Input and Objections Period.
- **Prioritization Draw in first half of 2027** — virtual, public, sets the order that applications and contention sets are processed after the String Evaluation Stage (expected 180 days, starts after the Community Input + Objections Period).

**Why Reveal Day matters for decentralized DNS** (this library's lens):

1. **The first verified picture of demand at the individual-string level**. Until now, the public discussion has been about *categories* of applications (`.web3`, `.wallet`, etc.) and the **1,616 number** (the 47 of 1,663 submitted apps that failed to pay the per-application fee). Reveal Day is the day when the actual entity → string mapping is published.
2. **The contention-set data is the first institutional pressure test for the alt-naming-system advocates**. If 5 different orgs apply for `.wallet`, the contention-set is the first time the alt-naming community can see the specific institutional pressure on a name that conflicts with an alt-naming-system convention. The TSG Initial Report (see §7) is the *policy* surface for this pressure; Reveal Day is the *factual* surface.
3. **The simultaneous Oct 7 (Reveal Day) + Oct 11 (KSK rollover) window is the most institutionally-loaded second week of October 2026 in the history of the global DNS** — as the W40 update predicted.
4. **The Sep 29 announcement moves the date a full week earlier than ICANNWiki's upper bound of Oct 14** (per the "9 weeks after Aug 12 application-window-close" rule of thumb). ICANN has not published a reason for the acceleration; the most likely explanation is the simultaneous Oct 11 KSK rollover — putting Reveal Day in the same week concentrates institutional attention.

**Per CircleID Sep 30**:
- "The date moves disclosure a week ahead of ICANN's previous outside estimate. ICANN said on September 22 that Reveal Day would occur no later than October 14, nine weeks after the application window closed."
- "A two-week replacement period begins hours after the disclosure. From 00:01 UTC on October 8 through 23:59 UTC on October 21, eligible applicants that submitted an alternate replacement string may switch from their primary string."
- "ICANN has also fixed **November 17 as String Confirmation Day**, when it will publish the final list of chosen strings and updated contention sets."

**The Sep 22 ICANN release** ([icann.org/en/announcements/details/icann-confirms-number-of-applications-proceeding-in-the-2026-round-22-09-2026-en](https://www.icann.org/en/announcements/details/icann-confirms-number-of-applications-proceeding-in-the-2026-round-22-09-2026-en)) — referenced in the W40 update — confirmed 1,616 paid applications + 47 unpaid. The Sep 29 announcement closes the upper-bound ambiguity by setting Oct 7 as the firm date.

**For this library**: Oct 7 is the day the alt-naming-system community can map the institutional pressure on the legacy DNS. **Watch the [ENS](/kb/ens/) / [Handshake](/kb/handshake/) / [Namecoin](/kb/namecoin/) social channels Oct 6-8** for reactions to the application-public-portion disclosures (which often include intended-use statements, market-segment claims, and target-user definitions that the alt-naming-system community can use to position themselves). **The W41 sweep's primary source for Oct 7+ is the APS website itself** — [newgtldprogram.icann.org](https://newgtldprogram.icann.org).

**Package versions**:
- pkarr: 8.1.0
- pkarr-relay: 2.1.0
- @synonymdev/pkarr: 8.1.0

**Why separate DHT/relay timeouts matter**:

The PKARR lookup architecture (since v3 / v4) is: a client tries to resolve a public key → it queries the Mainline DHT for the public key's signed resource record → if the DHT is slow or unresponsive, the client also tries the pkarr-relay cache → if the relay has a fresh answer, the client gets it fast. The two paths were gated by a **single timeout** in the previous API — meaning the client had to choose between "wait long enough for the DHT" and "fail fast and fall through to the relay". With separate timeouts:

- **The relay path can be set to fail fast (e.g., 1 second)** — the relay should always have a recent answer; if it doesn't respond in 1 second, the relay is degraded and the client should fall through to the DHT.
- **The DHT path can be set to wait longer (e.g., 10 seconds)** — the DHT can be slow under load, but it's the source of truth for the public key's signed record.

**The net effect is a faster, more reliable PKARR client** — the relay fast-path succeeds quickly when healthy, and the DHT slow-path has enough time budget to actually succeed when needed.

**For this library**: PKARR's first non-chore v8 release is structurally significant because **DHT-vs-relay path tuning is the operational surface that will define PKARR's reliability story as pubky-homeserver deployments scale**. The W40 weekly noted that "PKARR is now treated as a stable dependency", but stable ≠ feature-complete. v8.1.0 is the first v8 release that adds a **new client capability** rather than a chore / lockfile bump.

### 6. Root KSK rollover T-6 days to Oct 11 2026 + APNIC podcast Oct 1

**APNIC podcast** ([blog.apnic.net/2026/10/01/podcast-the-october-2026-root-ksk-roll](https://blog.apnic.net/2026/10/01/podcast-the-october-2026-root-ksk-roll)) — Duane Wessels on the rollover history with Roy Arends (ICANN):

- The **first KSK rollover in 2017** completed successfully.
- The **2020 second rollover was delayed** by unexpected circumstances — the trust-anchor rollback in 2017-2018 + the 2018-2019 invalid trust-anchor telemetry from multiple resolvers (an entire ecosystem of DNSSEC-validating resolvers was reading the trust anchor as "revoked" when it was actually "standby", which caused a global DNSSEC outage for some networks).
- The **2023 second-rollover attempt was delayed again** (postponed from Mar 2020 → Sep 2020 → Q1 2021 → "indefinite" by Oct 2018).
- **On Oct 11 2026 the new KSK (KSK-2024) begins signing the root zone** while KSK-2017 is retired from active signing operations (it stays visible in the root zone until Jan 2027 when it is marked revoked and removed per the [Infoblox Oct 2026 preparation blog](https://www.infoblox.com/blog/company/you-dont-need-a-locksmith-preparing-for-the-root-zone-key-signing-key-ksk-rollover-in-october-2026)).
- **DNSSEC-validating resolvers that don't update their trust-anchor configuration will fail DNSSEC validation on Oct 11.** The fix for a BIND-based resolver is to update `trusted-keys` or `managed-keys` to include the new KSK-2024 trust anchor.

**The ICANN-side KSK story**:

- **ICANN announced (Sep 24 per W40 + Aug 11 per [icann.org/resources/press-material/release-2026-08-11-en](https://www.icann.org/resources/press-material/release-2026-08-11-en))** that the new root KSK (KSK-2024) will become the active key on Oct 11 2026.
- The ICANN Board LA workshop (Sep 4-6) approved the rollover.
- The [LinkedIn post by comparethecloud-net on Oct 1](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/comparethecloud-net_icann-schedules-dns-root-key-replacement-activity-7470444688240578561-5d0A) confirms the date.
- ICANN's [@ICANN tweet Sep 24](https://x.com/ICANN/status/2092175222359105974) reminds operators that the new trust anchor KSK-2024 is the one to configure for Oct 11.

**Why KSK rollover matters for this library**:

1. **[Namecoin](/kb/namecoin/) ncp11 HTTPS-in-stock-browsers** chains through the conventional DNS root to validate the Namecoin chain's TLS cert. The Namecoin core team's recent work on Encasha (off-chain SSHFP records) and the ncp11 stack (DNSSEC chain for `.bit` HTTPS) is **operationally affected by the KSK rollover** — operators of Namecoin-anchored HTTPS sites need to ensure their DNSSEC-validating resolvers have the new KSK-2024 trust anchor configured by Oct 11.
2. **The DNSSEC chain is the load-bearing trust assumption for ENS DNSSEC imports** (the algorithm-13 imports that the W40 update tracked with estmcmxci.eth's P-256 precompile migration). A KSK rollover that breaks DNSSEC validation on a Namecoin-anchored chain would break ENS DNSSEC imports for any `.bit` name.
3. **The simultaneous Oct 7 (Reveal Day) + Oct 11 (KSK rollover) = institutionally-loaded second week of October 2026**, as the W40 update predicted.

**For this library**: DNSSEC trust-anchor + ENS testnet resolver address are both "load-bearing trust" events that the W41 sweep would miss if it only watched decentralized-DNS repos. **The P2P ODoH stack (#958 → #959 → #960)** is the Handshake-side privacy/DoH answer:

- **#958 (Jul 17)**: net: add experimental P2P DNS relay — base layer.
- **#959 (Jul 21)**: net: add experimental P2P ODoH relay (stacked on #958) — adds Oblivious DoH (ODoH, IETF RFC 9230) on top of the P2P DNS relay. ODoH separates the client IP from the DNS query — the resolver sees the query but not the client IP, and a proxy sees the client IP but not the query.
- **#960 (Jul 21)**: net: add experimental HNS node rendezvous and relay — the Handshake node discovery layer.

**The P2P ODoH stack is the Handshake-side privacy/DoH answer, mirroring the DNS-AID draft-02 strategy of bringing standard-DNS-anchor-amenable surface to non-ICANN identity**. ODoH is the IETF standard for resolver-hidden DNS lookups (RFC 9230, May 2022) — a Handshake node that runs an ODoH resolver can answer `.hns` queries without learning the client IP, which is a meaningful privacy upgrade over the existing Handshake resolver model.

**HIP018 Rerecord A and AAAA support** (#955, 25 comments, the most active HIP-anchored PR):

- The Handshake Improvement Proposal (HIP) #18 is about Rerecord — a re-record allows a Handshake name owner to update the resource record set (TLD records) for a name. The "Add narrow Rerecord A and AAAA support" PR is a **narrow Rerecord implementation that only allows A and AAAA records** (instead of all record types) — this is the smallest-increment Rerecord that adds practical value (lets a Handshake name point to an updated IP without burning a new name).

**For this library**: the P2P ODoH + HIP018 Rerecord A/AAAA is the **first material Handshake-ecosystem development since the airstop fork confirmed live (W40 update)** — but the work is by the Handshake community (Nathanwoodburn + denuoweb + shadstoneofficial) not the core handshake-org team. The community-vs-core gap is now 13 months. **The Handshake story is structurally bifurcated**: the supply side (airstop fork + ~890M HNS permanently burned) is settled and irreversible, but the protocol-development side is dependent on community PRs rather than core-team shipping.
