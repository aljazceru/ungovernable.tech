#### What Was Approved (Narrowed Scope)

The executable proposal was significantly scaled back from the June 19 temp check after community criticism (led by the original constitution author and others) about treasury concentration:

- **Foundation becomes operating entity**: Five-seat board with full-time Executive Director, authorized to administer the Endowment (~$65M managed by Karpatkey).
- **1M ENS allocation**: One-time transfer to the Foundation for employee compensation, multi-year vesting. This is the only ENS token transaction in the proposal.
- **DAO retains**: Operational wallet (wallet.ensdao.eth, ~$16M in ETH and stablecoins) and ~54.6M ENS tokens remain under tokenholder control via existing on-chain mechanisms.
- **Protocol governance stays with tokenholders**: Contract upgrades, registry, fees, constitution changes — all remain DAO-governed.

#### What Was Removed (vs. June Temp Check)

The temp check proposed delegating management of the DAO operational wallet, ENS token holdings, and Endowment to the Foundation. The executable version:
- Keeps the operational wallet with the DAO (existing streams continue).
- Keeps DAO-held ENS tokens under tokenholder control (minus the 1M transfer).
- Only the Endowment moves to Foundation administration.

#### Freedom-Tech Analysis

The "Next Era" marks ENS's definitive transition from DAO-driven experiment to institutional-grade infrastructure. The Mozilla/Signal/Linux Foundation model is explicitly cited. This is a double-edged sword for sovereignty:

- **Positive**: Professionalized operations, legal entity for partnerships (ICANN, governments), sustainable funding for development. ENS as infrastructure is more robust.
- **Negative**: Concentration of operational authority in five people (two from ENS Labs). The $65M Endowment under Foundation control is significant. While tokenholders retain protocol governance, the day-to-day reality of a Foundation-run organization is different from a DAO-run one.
- **Context**: ENS's market cap (~$169-191M circulating) is less than half its treasury (~$400M+). This gap has fueled the governance debate — the treasury is the elephant in the room.

The integrationist path continues: ENS works *within* ICANN rather than against it. The `.ens` gTLD application is the practical expression of this strategy.

#### Namespace Collision Risk

ICANN may delegate strings (.wallet, .crypto, .nft) that blockchain registries already sold as Web3-only domains. Holders of affected blockchain domains face resolution risk — their "domain" may collide with an ICANN-delegated TLD they don't control. This validates key-rooted approaches (PKARR, Tor .onion) that don't claim TLD strings.

### 3. PKARR v8.0.0 — DHT Decoupling

The core PKARR library shipped a major version on August 6, 2026.

#### Breaking Changes

1. **DHT API decoupled from Mainline**: `refactor(dht)!: decouple public API from mainline` (PR #287). The public PKARR API no longer directly exposes Mainline DHT internals — a `DhtClient` abstraction layer now sits between them. This improves API stability when the underlying `mainline` crate changes.
2. **`mainline` upgraded to v8** (PR #285): Bumps the underlying DHT library. The DHT protocol itself (BEP44) is unchanged — records remain compatible.
3. **Relay testnet support gated behind explicit configuration** (PR #287): Relay operators must now opt-in to testnet support. This prevents accidental testnet traffic on production relays.
4. **`simple-dns` upgraded to 0.12**: DNS record parsing library updated. Downstream consumers may need to adjust record construction.

#### What Didn't Change

- Ed25519 signing and BEP44 mutable items remain the same.
- DNS packet format unchanged (1000-byte limit persists).
- Mainline DHT network (~10M nodes) unchanged.
- Records remain backward-compatible with v7/v6 resolvers.

#### Release Cadence

| Version | Date | Key Change |
|---------|------|------------|
| v6.0.1 | May 27 2026 | rustls fix |
| v7.0.0 | Jul 17 2026 | Resolve policies, structured errors, relay rate limiting, DhtClient abstraction |
| **v8.0.0** | **Aug 6 2026** | **Mainline v8, DHT API decoupling, relay testnet gating** |

v7 → v8 in three weeks indicates rapid iteration on the relay and DHT layer. The resolve-policy system (CacheOnly, CacheFirst, NetworkOnly) introduced in v7 is now the standard resolution interface.

#### Significance

PKARR is maturing from an experimental library into infrastructure. The API decoupling means downstream consumers (Pubky, Iroh, pkdns) can upgrade the DHT layer without breaking their own APIs. The relay rate-limiting improvements (HTTP, inbound DHT, and user-triggered DHT quotas split in v7) position PKARR relays for production load.

**New Features**:

### 7. ICANN Root KSK Rollover — October 11 Preparation

ICANN continues its preparation campaign for the October 11, 2026 root zone KSK rollover. Key resources published:

- **Blog post (Jul 27)**: "Preparing for the Root Zone KSK Rollover: What You Need to Know" — historical context (2010 signing, 2018 first rollover), KSK-2024 adoption data (>95% of validating resolvers ready), and critical steps for administrators.
- **"What to Expect" PDF (Jul 27)**: Detailed technical document covering which users may be affected (small percentage), what root server operators will see, and the phased timeline.
- **CircleID analysis**: 2024-2026 rollover shows "very similar trends" to the 2017-2018 rollover. Both show >95% adoption of the incoming key after ~500 days of pre-publication.

#### Timeline

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| Jan 2025 | KSK-2024 pre-published in root zone (21 months prep) |
| **Oct 11 2026** | **Root zone begins using only KSK-2024** |
| Jan 2027 | Current KSK retired |

#### What Happens If You're Not Ready

DNSSEC-validating resolvers without KSK-2024 in their trust anchor set will experience **total DNS resolution failure** — cutting off internet access for users behind those resolvers. This affects operators of self-sovereign infrastructure (pkdns, GNS proxies, custom unbound/knot instances).

#### Algorithm Rollover Proposal (Separate Track)

ICANN also published a "Proposed Root KSK Algorithm Rollover" for public comment (Feb 3, 2026). The root zone has used RSA/SHA-256 since 2010. This proposal establishes a process for eventually transitioning to a different signing algorithm (potentially post-quantum). This is a long-term track, not the October 2026 event.

### 8. DNS-AID — Naming for the Agentic Web (Continuing)

No new DNS-AID developments this week, but the standardization effort remains active and critical for the freedom-tech community to monitor.

**Current Status**:
- IETF Internet-Draft: `draft-mozleywilliams-dnsop-dnsaid-01` (March 2026, expires September 2026).
- Linux Foundation project (launched May 27, 2026) with Python SDK, CLI, and MCP server reference implementation.
- Backers: Cloudflare, GoDaddy, Equinix, Infoblox, ISC, CSC, Indeed, WWT.
- Academic foundation: arXiv 2606.02314.
- Companion: Agent Name Service (ANS) for verification.

**Freedom-Tech Gap**: DNS-AID defines how AI agents discover each other using existing DNS infrastructure. If this becomes the standard, agent identity is anchored to DNS — meaning ICANN, CAs, and registrars remain choke points. PKARR-based agent discovery (self-sovereign, DHT-resolved, no DNS dependency) is technically viable but absent from the standardization conversation. The IETF DAWN working list is the venue for engagement.

## Freedom-Tech Takeaways & Recommendations

1. **ENS Has Chosen Its Path**: With the "Next Era" proposal passed, ENS is now definitively an institution — Foundation-operated, ICANN-engaged, governance-professionalized. This is not a sovereignty project anymore. It's infrastructure with institutional backing. For public-facing identity that needs mainstream recognition (wallets, dApps, institutional use), ENS remains the best option. For seizure-proof pseudonymous naming, look elsewhere (PKARR, GNS, Tor).

2. **PKARR Is the Only Stack with Consumer-Momentum**: pubky-app receives daily commits. No other decentralized naming project has a consumer-facing application under active development at this pace. The homeserver rename, v0.10 stable, pkarr v8, and the `/priv` private storage alpha indicate the stack is coalescing into a complete platform: naming + identity + social + private storage, all on the DHT.

3. **The gTLD Window Is Closing — Watch the Collision Zone**: After August 12, we'll know which strings ICANN received applications for. The collision risk (blockchain TLDs vs. ICANN TLDs) becomes concrete. Monitor which Web3 TLD strings get applied for — if `.crypto`, `.wallet`, `.nft` get ICANN applicants, existing blockchain domain holders are in jeopardy.

4. **October 11 KSK Rollover — Test Your Stack**: If you run pkdns, a GNS proxy, unbound, or any DNSSEC-validating resolver, verify KSK-2024 is in your trust anchor. This is operational hygiene. A self-sovereign resolver that breaks on KSK rollover undermines the sovereignty claim.

5. **The Agent Naming Gap Widens**: DNS-AID continues at the Linux Foundation with IETF standardization. The freedom-tech community has not engaged. Every week without a PKARR-based agent discovery proposal is a week closer to agent identity being permanently DNS-anchored. This is the naming battle of the next decade — and the window for alternative input is closing.

6. **Handshake's Value Is Now Purely Architectural**: No commercial adoption, no browser support, token at ATL. The only argument for Handshake is architectural purity — you own your TLD on-chain. This is a valid but narrow use case. For most operators, PKARR or GNS serve better.

7. **Namecoin's Longevity Is Its Own Argument**: 15 years of operation, tracking Bitcoin Core, no protocol failures. The `.bit` stack (ncp11 + ncdns + ncgencert) works in stock browsers. It's not exciting, but it works. The longest-running blockchain DNS experiment in history.
