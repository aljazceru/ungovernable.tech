## Akash Network — $5M Monthly Spend, BME Live, Homenode Early Access

The most consequential quarter in Akash's history. [Akash](/kb/akash/) crossed **$5M monthly compute spend** in Q1 2026, a record driven by Mainnet 17's Burn-Mint Equilibrium (BME) mechanism and the AkashML inference platform hitting **1.7 billion tokens/day on OpenRouter**.

### Burn-Mint Equilibrium (BME) — Live March 23, 2026

BME is Akash's answer to a fundamental DePIN tension: stable pricing for enterprises vs. token demand. The mechanism:

```
User payment → buys AKT on open market → AKT burned → produces ACT (non-transferable stablecoin)
ACT settles transaction with providers
Providers mint ACT back to AKT at market price
```

- Every compute transaction now burns AKT, creating deflationary pressure as usage grows.
- Providers receive stable ACT (not volatile AKT), satisfying enterprise settlement needs.
- Tenants can pay in USDC via AEP-23 (already live), while BME creates AKT demand from the settlement layer.
- 250+ participants stress-tested BME on testnet before mainnet launch (March 23, 2026).
- Post-launch data: compute spend acceleration to $5M/month confirms the thesis.
- Governance: AEP-76 (BME) was approved; Proposal 318 ratified mainnet activation.

This is the most technically significant tokenomics change in any major DePIN compute network in 2026.

### AEP-65: Confidential Computing with TEE — Target July 2026

[Akash](/kb/akash/) is adding TEE attestation support via AEP-65. This would make Akash (via provider-attested workloads) composable with the [Phala and 0G](/kb/phala-and-0g/) and [Confidential-AI-Inference](/kb/confidential-ai-inference/) stack. When live:

- Provider operators cannot read tenant workloads.
- Attestation reports prove hardware integrity.
- Complements (rather than replaces) the Akash + Phala dstack hybrid pattern documented in existing files.

| Infrastructure | Procurement Timeline |
|---|---|
| Transformers | Up to 4 years |
| Nuclear permits | 10–15 years |
| Siemens Energy turbines | Sold out for 7 years |

Key implications:

- AI inference demand is growing faster than energy infrastructure can follow.

This is the energy-supply argument for decentralized compute — distinct from the censorship-resistance and sovereignty arguments, but compelling for a different threat model (infrastructure collapse rather than governance capture).

### Yuma Consensus and Validator Dynamics

The April 2026 "Navigating Bittensor" community writeup surfaced a governance crisis — a large validator operator pulled out of three subnets, dumped $10M in TAO, and exited. This illustrates Bittensor's ongoing tension:

- Stake-weighted validators have significant power over subnet emissions.
- Concentration risk: a small number of validator entities control scoring on most subnets.
- dTAO (dynamic TAO, launched 2024) created per-subnet derivative tokens — subnets compete on market valuation, not just technical merit.

For this library's purposes: Bittensor is the reference implementation of **token-coordinated AI infrastructure at production scale**, with all the trade-offs that implies. It is not a sovereignty primitive — validator concentration is a real attack surface — but it demonstrates that decentralized AI markets can sustain billions in economic activity.

## Golem — Salad.com Engineering Trial

[Golem](/kb/golem/) announced a partnership with **Salad.com** (a GPU cloud platform) in January 2026 for an engineering trial integrating Web2 workloads with Golem's decentralized compute via GLM settlement on Polygon.

This is Golem's path to relevance in 2026: not competing with Phala on confidential AI or Akash on GPU inventory, but serving enterprises that want GLM-denominated compute for batch workloads with Polygon settlement costs.

## Nosana — GPU Grid Testnet Phase 1 Concluded

Nosana concluded the **first phase of public GPU grid testing**. Key findings from the inaugural test grid:

- Node identification, selection, and assignment pipeline validated.
- Diverse AI inference workloads tested.
- Feedback loop informing Phase 2 improvements.
- Documented partnerships: Exabits, Piknik, Inferia, Sogni, Rivalz, OpenGPU.

Nosana positions itself as purpose-built for AI on Solana — closer to io.net than to Akash in architecture. The Solana DePIN stack (Render, io.net, Nosana) is forming a coherent alternative to Cosmos-based compute (Akash) and Substrate-based compute (Phala/Bittensor).

## Summary: The DePIN Compute Landscape Mid-2026

The field is consolidating around three architectural poles:

| Pole | Projects | Trust Model | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Solana DePIN GPU** | Render, io.net, Nosana | Provider stake, no TEE | GPU inventory, fast settlement |
| **Cosmos DePIN** | Akash (AKT), Bittensor-adjacent | Stake + escrow | Kubernetes flexibility, BME tokenomics |
| **TEE-attested** | Phala / dstack, 0G | Hardware attestation (TDX/SEV/H100 CC) | Confidential AI, cryptographic trust |
| **Verifier/scorer markets** | Bittensor | Yuma consensus (EigenTrust) | AI task marketplace, economic scale |

The most important developments for this library's threat model:

1. **Akash BME** — deflationary tokenomics tied to real compute spend is a new quality tier for DePIN.
2. **Homenode** — consumer GPU decentralization is now live, not theoretical.
3. **AEP-65 (July 2026)** — Akash + TEE composition eliminates the last major trust gap.
4. **Phala's 10,004 total users, 398 paid users, 2,529 CVMs** (end-2025) — TEE confidential compute has a real production user base.
5. **Greg Osuri's energy constraint argument** — infrastructure collapse is a distinct threat model that decentralized compute addresses better than any alternative.
