6. **ePrint 2026/2113 (Sept 25, Branco/Döttling/Müller-Quade/Quan) — "Post-Quantum PKE and More from a Noisy Unstructured Linear Algebraic Assumption: Beyond LWE and LPN"** is the most consequential cryptanalytic result of the week for freedom-tech. The paper introduces an NLA (noisy linear algebraic assumption) that is **plausibly hard given an oracle that breaks even constant-noise-rate LPN** (a strictly stronger LPN break than current lattice attacks provide), while remaining plausibly hard given an LWE oracle. The result: a single new natural assumption yields (i) public-key encryption, (ii) two-round maliciously secure oblivious transfer, and (iii) one-time circular-secure SKE. The relevance for freedom-tech: **diversification beyond the lattice-only assumption of ML-KEM/ML-DSA** — the paper is the first to make a single, structured assumption viable for all three primitives while explicitly being **not** LPN-broken. The paper's cryptanalysis rules out (1) combinatorial ISD-style attacks, (2) dimension reduction, (3) dual lattice attacks. **Important boundary:** this is a new hardness assumption; it is *not* a threat to ML-KEM, ML-DSA, or SLH-DSA; it is a diversification track.

**Notable**: the ML-KEM BMI2+AVX2 gate is the most operationally consequential PQ change in the release. AWS-LC's ML-KEM C reference implementation is CAVP-validated; the AVX2 fast path is a performance optimization that has historically been gated on runtime detection of AVX2 support. **The fix tightens that gate to also require BMI2**, because the AVX2 implementation uses BMI2 instructions (specifically `_pdep_u32` / `_pext_u32`) and would fail to link or produce undefined behavior on silicon that lacks BMI2. The fix is correct, but operators on legacy x86-64 hardware should verify the fallback path is being exercised as expected (no perf surprise). For embedded and IoT paths, this is a non-event (most embedded targets are ARMv8 and use aarch64 codepaths, not x86-64).

### 4. ePrint 2026/2113 (Sept 25) — the diversification track beyond LWE/LPN

The Branco-Döttling-Müller-Quade-Quan paper *"Post-Quantum PKE and More from a Noisy Unstructured Linear Algebraic Assumption: Beyond LWE and LPN"* (ePrint [2026/2113](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2113)) is the most consequential new cryptographic-assumption paper of the week for freedom-tech. The construction's key property: **the NLA is plausibly hard against an oracle that breaks even constant-noise-rate LPN**, while still being plausibly hard against an LWE oracle.

**Why this matters for freedom-tech**: the lattice-only assumption of ML-KEM and ML-DSA has been the single load-bearing assumption of the NIST PQC standardization. The Sept 25 [note](/briefings/post-quantum/2026-09-26/)'s Mattsson recommendation (ML-KEM + HQC hybrid for diversification) was the operational workaround — but HQC is also lattice-derived (code-based, with a lattice-closest reduction), so the diversification is partial. 2026/2113 opens a path to an NLA-derived PKE primitive that is provably not in the LPN-broken regime even if LWE weakens. **For a deployer fielding PQC on the wire, the practical implication is: in 12-24 months, there will be additional PKE candidates worth evaluating that are structurally distinct from lattices. Track this paper for replication and implementation maturity.**

**Important boundary**: 2026/2113 introduces a *new* hardness assumption; it does not break ML-KEM, ML-DSA, or SLH-DSA. The paper's contribution is in the *assumption landscape*, not in the *primitive landscape*. Deployers should not delay ML-KEM / ML-DSA / SLH-DSA adoption in anticipation of a 2026/2113-derived primitive — the standardization-and-deployment gap would be 3-5 years even on an accelerated path. The paper's value is in *expanding the diversification menu* for long-lived deployments that want non-lattice alternatives.

### 5. ePrint 2026/1992 (Sept 23) — Google 2029 deadline formalized

The Khurana et al. paper *"Bridging the Cryptographic Transition: A Unified Framework for Post-Quantum Key Exchange"* (ePrint [2026/1992](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1992)) introduces a formal security framework for migrating classical KEX to PQ KEX, framed against the *Google 2029 internal PQC migration deadline*. The deadline is the central research framing: Google has established 2029 (deliberately ahead of the 2035 NIST/federal horizon) as the year for completing PQC migration in its production stack. The motivation, per the paper: faster-than-expected progress in (a) quantum computing hardware, (b) quantum error correction, and (c) revised resource estimates for quantum computers to break current cryptographic schemes.

**For freedom-tech**: the 2029 Google deadline is the first *private-sector* PQC deadline published by a hyperscaler. The NIST/federal horizon is 2030/2031/2035 (Phase 3/4/5 per OMB M-26-15); the 2030/2031 dates are for *priority* systems (HVAs, high-impact), not the full estate. The 2035 date is the backstop for the rest. Google’s 2029 target is a migration deadline. It cannot be used to infer a three-year interval between recording traffic and a future quantum decryption capability. **The paper's unified framework gives a reduction-style argument that the hybrid composition (classical KEM + PQ KEM) implemented in RFC 10024 is security-preserving under the standard IND-CCA assumptions on both components**. Cross-reference [Hybrid KEM Migration](/guides/hybrid-kem-migration/).

- [ ] **No action items from the IETF re-block**: the KEM-then-compose architecture is the IETF's documented path; the dispute is about specific document publication, not about the cryptographic primitives. - [ ] **No action items on the Dihedral Coset line**: the August 15 GRZ refutation stands; ML-KEM and ML-DSA are not moving.
- [ ] **No action items on the September cryptanalysis**: 2026/2137 (single-trace ML-KEM key-recovery, q-randomization countermeasure) and 2026/2131 (RSA-4096 below 128-bit under signing-oracle) remain the two papers that matter for high-value-deployer planning. For embedded / side-channel-exposed deployments, the q-randomization countermeasure (937 bytes firmware) restores ~4 bits of security loss instead of >140. Continue moving away from RSA for long-lived signing keys; hybrid Ed25519+ML-DSA-65 per [Hybrid KEM Migration](/guides/hybrid-kem-migration/).
- [ ] **ML-KEM + HQC hybrid** is the most explicitly-named diversification strategy on the standards track (per Mattsson's Sept 25 pqc-forum post). For operators with the freedom to deploy non-finalized primitives, this is the standards-track-aligned diversification path. HQC standardization is expected 2026-2027; operators can field HQC hybrid before the standard finalizes. Cross-reference [HQC](/kb/hqc/).
- [ ] **MIKE (isogeny-based, research-grade)** can be fielded in operator-controlled settings where only isogeny-based options fit (per Obenour's Sept 25 forum post). Hybridize with ECDH and Kerberos-style symmetric keying so security holds if any component holds.
- [ ] **ePrint 2026/2113 (NLA beyond LWE/LPN)**: track for replication. Do not delay ML-KEM / ML-DSA / SLH-DSA adoption; treat 2026/2113 as a 12-24-month diversification track.
- [ ] **ePrint 2026/1992 (Google 2029)**: the 2029 date is a migration target, not a forecast of when recorded traffic becomes decryptable. The unified-framework result reinforces the hybrid-by-default operational recommendation.
