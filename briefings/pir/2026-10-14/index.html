SkrrtPIR (ePrint 2026/2385, Oct 6)

Title: SkrrtPIR: Doubly-Stateless Batch PIR from Single-Key RLWE Unpacking Authors: Keewoo Lee (Ethereum Foundation, ORCID 0000-0002-2521-8538) + Yongha Son (Sungshin Women's University, ORCID 0000-0003-1394-234X) ePrint: https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2385 IACR news item: https://iacr.org/news/item/30019 (Oct 8) History: 2026-10-08 approved, 2026-10-06 received Category: Cryptographic protocols

What the paper does: initiates the study of doubly-stateless Batch PIR, where the server stores no per-client state and the client maintains no database-dependent hint. Prior concretely-efficient Batch PIR schemes (Bienstock et al. USENIX Security 2024; Patel, Persiano, Seo, Yeo S&P 2026) require per-client server state (client-specific evaluation keys), which makes queries linkable across sessions and complicates deployment. The natural baseline of "send fresh evaluation keys with each batch query" is the comparison point.

The technical contribution: a single-key RLWE unpacking procedure that replaces the traditional tree-based approach (which requires logarithmically many Galois keys) with a linear walk over a cyclic Galois subgroup requiring only a single Galois key. This is the key-engineering step: the tree-based approach is the bottleneck for concrete deployment (each tree level adds a key, and the tree is logarithmic in the number of batch entries).

Why this matters: - Ethereum Foundation PIR research line is now publishing at the rate of one paper per week: 2026/1959 (Sep 13, the 2-server IT-PIR 6·log n lower bound — the first tightening of the Wehner–de Wolf 5·log n bound in 20 years) → 2026/2412 (Oct 8, Barely Sublinear RAM-FHE from Plain LWE) → 2026/2385 (Oct 6, SkrrtPIR). Three papers in 23 days from the same primary author. The Sep 30 sweep's "the W42 cycle's most-likely iacr.org paper is the Keewoo Lee 2-server IT-PIR" was the wrong paper — SkrrtPIR + RAM-FHE are both 2026 Keewoo Lee papers, just not the lower bound. - The "doubly-stateless" property maps directly to wallet-deployment constraints — the wallet ↔ RPC-provider relationship has no natural place to store per-client server state (the wallet is the only persistent client), and the wallet's storage budget is bounded by the mobile-device envelope. SkrrtPIR is the first Batch PIR that does not require a client-side database-dependent hint and does not require server-side per-client state — i.e., it is the first Batch PIR that is "Tiptoe-compatible" (Tiptoe's per-client state is the 57 MiB preprocessed DB; SkrrtPIR requires no per-client state at all). - The cyclic-subgroup trick is the same Galois-subgroup walk that ReinsPIRe (2026/1934, Sep 8) uses to avoid NTT in most operations; the technique is now appearing in three independent Keewoo Lee papers (2026/1934 / 2026/2385 / 2026/2412), suggesting the Lee + Ethereum-Foundation line is now standardized on cyclic-subgroup walks as the key-engineering primitive. - The Yongha Son co-authorship is a Sungshin Women's University → Ethereum Foundation collaboration; this is a relatively rare faculty → foundation pipeline. The Sungshin connection is a different institutional axis from the prior Ethereum Foundation PIR line (which was all-Ethereum-Foundation until now).

Barely Sublinear RAM-FHE from Plain LWE (ePrint 2026/2412, Oct 8)

Title: Barely Sublinear RAM-FHE from Plain LWE Author: Keewoo Lee (Ethereum Foundation, ORCID 0000-0002-2521-8538) — sole author ePrint: https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/2412 History: 2026-10-11 approved, 2026-10-08 received Category: Foundations

What the paper does: constructs a RAM-FHE scheme (FHE for RAM programs, Hamlin, Holmgren, Weiss, Wichs Crypto 2019) that evaluates each step of a RAM program in o(N/log N) time from plain (unstructured) LWE. The prior construction (Lin, Mook, Wichs STOC 2023) used Ring-LWE and is "the only construction from a concrete assumption" — this paper is the first from plain LWE.

The "barely" matters: the per-step cost is o(N/log N) — "barely sublinear" — but the saving over the linear cost of standard FHE was not known to be achievable from plain LWE. The point is the assumption weakening (Ring-LWE → plain LWE), not the asymptotic gain.

The PIR bridge: as LMW did, this work builds RAM-FHE by extending a Doubly-Efficient PIR (DEPIR) scheme. DEPIR is the natural primitive for this construction because DEPIR is exactly the cryptographic primitive that supports sublinear-time RAM access: a DEPIR client preprocesses the database once (offline), then makes online queries that are sublinear in the database size. The PIR ↔ RAM-FHE bridge is the LMW 2023 construction pattern; this paper extends the pattern from Ring-LWE to plain LWE.

The CRS-removal step: the only DEPIR from plain LWE — Lee's CRYPTO 2026 paper — relied on a Common Reference String (CRS) that is incompatible with the RAM-FHE extension. The paper's contribution is the CRS-removal — i.e., the paper's primary technical work is in DEPIR, and the RAM-FHE construction is the natural application. The DEPIR-from-plain-LWE in the standard model may be of independent interest (CRYPTO 2026 Lee's DEPIR was the first plain-LWE DEPIR; this paper's DEPIR is the first plain-LWE DEPIR in the standard model).

Why this matters: - Plain LWE is the conservative post-quantum assumption. Ring-LWE has a known history of attacks (subfield attacks, overstretched-NTRU); plain LWE is the assumption that has held up to the longest sustained cryptanalytic effort. A RAM-FHE from plain LWE is the most conservatively post-quantum-secure RAM-FHE to date. - The DEPIR-from-plain-LWE in the standard model is the fourth DEPIR in the literature, after YPIR (USENIX Security 2024), LMW (STOC 2023), and Lee's CRYPTO 2026. The proliferation of DEPIR primitives is the result of the 2024–2026 burst in PIR research; the DEPIR model is now the canonical offline-online PIR formulation. - The "barely" / "may be of independent interest" language is conservative cryptographer-speak for "this is the result we wanted to land" — the paper's author considered the DEPIR-from-plain-LWE-in-the-standard-model the primary contribution, and the RAM-FHE is the application. - The Ethereum Foundation is the sole supporting institution for the paper's primary author — Keewoo Lee is the only listed author. The "barely" and the sole-authorship are signals of a single-author deep dive, not a multi-institution research program.
