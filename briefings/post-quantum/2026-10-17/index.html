  7. ePrint 2026/2402 (Oct 7) breaks a Crypto'20 lattice blind signature — Yi-Fu Lai + Yu Yu (SJTU). The paper Revisiting Lattice-based Blind Signatures Again shows an attack on a lattice-based blind signature proposed at Crypto'20.   8. ePrint 2026/2091 (W41, Sep 28) — ElGhamrawy et al. — soft-analytic approach to ILWE cuts ML-DSA secret recovery from 139M to 2.25M signatures. The paper Keep Track of Your Errors: Solving ILWE and Improving Attacks on ML-DSA (ElGhamrawy/Eisenbarth/Hermelink/Rabich/Sieck/Streit/Thietke/Zhang — U-Luebeck/DFKI/MPI-SP/Fraunhofer AISEC/TUM/RUB) shows that the number of signatures required for ML-DSA side-channel secret-recovery attacks has been drastically overestimated — distribution hints (Eurocrypt 2025) can be derived from Integer LWE (ILWE) samples and a belief-propagation-based solver recovers the secret key efficiently. In a noise-free attack setting, the approach reduces the average number of signatures required by a factor of 62 — from 139 million to 2.25 million — compared with linear regression, with larger improvements under noise. The paper also analyzes a recently discovered timing leakage in ML-DSA and shows how to solve for the secret key with far fewer signatures. The paper is the post-FIPS-204 side-channel reality check: ML-DSA implementations are not side-channel safe in the absence of explicit masking / constant-time / verification-of-implementations discipline, and the threshold for practical key recovery is now in the low-millions, not hundreds of millions. The Week in Detail

1. OpenSSL 4.1.0-beta2 — DTLS 1.3 ships with two known security issues, 4.1.0 final pushed

The OpenSSL 4.1.0-beta2 release was tagged on 2026-10-09T12:22:55Z and announced via openssl-announce the same day at 11:43:56 UTC. The release is the second pre-release of the 4.1 line, and carries the largest PQC CPU-side optimization in any OpenSSL 4.x release:

  • Optimized ML-DSA and ML-KEM NTT operations on ppc64le (POWER9 / POWER10 hardware)
  • Optimized ML-DSA operations on x86_64
  • AVX-512-optimized SHAKE x4 operations for ML-DSA on x86_64 (4-way SHAKE using AVX-512 to accelerate the matrix expansion in ML-DSA signing)
  • AVX-512 and VAES optimizations for AES-CBC decryption on x86_64
  • DTLS 1.3 (RFC 9147) support + ossl-guide-dtlsv13(7) manual page
  • RFC 8701 GREASE (Generate Random Extensions And Sustain Extensibility)
  • DTLS in the SSL listener API
  • IKEv2 KDF
  • Initial Elbrus2000 (e2k) architecture support (Russian Elbrus 2000 CPUs)

The release was forced to beta2 (rather than final) by two security issues in the new DTLS 1.3 code:

"These potential security issues in the DTLS-1.3 code were reported today. We did not want to release 4.1.0 with known security issues in the new implementation. For that reason, we have released 4.1.0-beta2 instead." — OpenSSL 4.1.0-beta2 release announcement, Oct 9

The two security issues are tracked as OpenSSL #33172 and #33173. The 4.1.0 final-release target in the OpenSSL 4.1 release-planning blog ("4.1.0 final: October 2026") is therefore broken — the final release is now gated on the DTLS 1.3 security fixes, with the earliest realistic date being November 2026 and a realistic late-Q4 / early-Q1-2027 window.

The DTLS 1.3 KeyUpdate retransmission buffer drop bug from beta1 (OpenSSL #32878) is also still open — when a DTLS 1.3 KeyUpdate is received, all other outstanding post-handshake records are dropped from the retransmission buffer, meaning post-handshake records still awaiting an ACK (such as a NewSessionTicket) will no longer be retransmitted if the original transmission is lost. A fix is in progress and planned for a future release.

For freedom-tech deployers planning 4.1 adoption in late 2026 / early 2027: 4.1 is the feature release that will absorb DTLS 1.3 + PQC optimization work — AVX-512 SHAKE for ML-DSA, ppc64le ML-DSA/ML-KEM NTT, s390x ML-DSA — and the first OpenSSL release with the DTLS 1.3 datagram protocol in the stack. OpenSSL 3.4 EOL is 2026-10-22 — same date as M-26-15 — and agencies still on the 3.4 LTS line must plan for end-of-life immediately after the M-26-15 deadline lands. The 3.5 LTS is supported through 2030-04-08; 4.0 is supported through 2027-05-14; 3.6 EOL 2026-11-01 is now 15 days out.

The OpenSSL 4.2 master-branch changes (the development branch post-4.1) are visible in the master CHANGES.md and include:

  • TLS 1.3 client PSK offer conditional on cipher-share digest + 0-RTT only when actually usable (Viktor Dukhovni)
  • MLKEM512X25519 and SecP256r1MLKEM512 hybrid TLS KEMs for the newly assigned IANA codepoints per draft-rosomakho-tls-ecdhe-mlkem512-00 (Viktor Dukhovni) — the first 4.x-branch hybrid with ML-KEM-512 (the smallest, lowest-latency parameter set) rather than the standard X25519MLKEM768
  • FIPS provider fips-indicator gettable context parameter that returns 1 for approved operations (Shane Lontis and Paul Dale) — for the OpenSSL 4.x FIPS 140-3 module, which is the next FIPS validation target after the AWS-LC FIPS 3.x / 4.x modules
  • AVX-512 and VAES optimizations for AES-CTR mode (2.9× to 3.9× speedup for inputs ≥ 1024 bytes) (Madan Mohan Manokar)
  • ASCON-AEAD128 cipher as specified in NIST SP 800-232 — the NIST-selected lightweight AEAD

The MLKEM512X25519 addition is significant for constrained environments and post-quantum agility: ML-KEM-512 has smaller keys (800-byte public, 1632-byte private, 768-byte ciphertext) than ML-KEM-768 (1184 / 2400 / 1088) at the cost of lower security margin (≈ 118-bit vs 192-bit). For high-volume REST APIs or embedded TLS clients where the ML-KEM-768 size overhead is the issue, ML-KEM-512 is the IETF codepoint to watch.

2. IACR CHES 2026 + LightSec 2026: the lattice standardization side-channel reality check

CHES 2026 (Oct 11–15, Antalya, Turkey) is the IACR hardware-PQC primary venue. The CHES program covers FPGA/ASIC acceleration of lattice-based KEMs and signatures, side-channel attacks on ML-KEM implementations, and hardware-software co-design for PQ primitives. The CHES 2026 program page is JavaScript-only and did not render to text in the harvest window — the full paper list is available as JSON but per-paper abstracts were not reachable.

LightSec 2026 (the 7th International Workshop on Lightweight Cryptography for Security & Privacy, Oct 11, Antalya, official affiliated event of CHES 2026) is the venue for the most important PQC side-channel paper of the week:

SEALSQ "Cross-information lattice attack on ML-KEM" — combines profiling-based techniques with lattice reduction against the ML-KEM key-generation encoding step. The attack recovers sensitive information under realistic conditions. The work pairs the attack with practical countermeasures and complements SEALSQ's QS7001 secure element, which is undergoing Common Criteria EAL5+ certification. The peer-reviewed paper is the first third-party confirmation of the Bernstein / June 2026 ML-DSA "exploiting bugs" thesis applied to ML-KEM — both lattice-based standardized algorithms are now formally documented as carrying side-channel fragility.

TCHES 2027 pre-prints cluster (papers appearing in the IACR Transactions on Cryptographic Hardware and Embedded Systems 2027 volume, surfaced as pre-prints in this window) include:

  • ePrint 2026/2474 Software-Pipelining The Number Theoretic Transform on Neural Processing Units: A Loop-Fission Methodology (Vermeersch/Pottier/Norga/de Ruijter/Verbauwhede — KU Leuven) — first systematic NTT acceleration on AMD AI Engine NPUs using manual loop fission. The work develops efficient Barrett reduction primitives and constructs three high-throughput, low-latency NTT implementations (radix-2, radix-4, and radix-8), achieving significant performance gains over the AIE compiler and prior literature. On AMD's first-generation AIE specifically, speedups reach ~7-10× in throughput and ~5-7× in latency per AIE tile compared with prior work. Supports arbitrary NTT-friendly moduli and polynomial sizes up to degree 4096 for 14-bit moduli and 2048 for 30-bit moduli. The paper is TCHES 2027-bound (publication info: "Published by the IACR in TCHES 2027"). For freedom-tech: NPUs are a fast-growing class of AI accelerators (AMD AI Engine, Apple Neural Engine, Qualcomm Hexagon NPU, Google Edge TPU); the work shows that NPUs are a viable third-class hardware accelerator for lattice PQC after GPUs and FPGAs — relevant for edge ML + PQC co-acceleration on AMD Ryzen AI laptops and servers.

For freedom-tech: the SEALSQ LightSec 2026 result is the week's most consequential deployment-relevant PQC paper, because it formally documents that both lattice-based NIST standards (ML-KEM for KEM, ML-DSA for signatures) carry side-channel fragility comparable to classical ECC — an order-of-magnitude change from the pre-2026 expectation that "lattice PQC is side-channel easy because the arithmetic is regular". Combined with ePrint 2026/2091 (item 8 above), the week's side-channel literature is the post-FIPS-203/204 reality check: the lattice-PQC side-channel surface is comparable to classical ECC, and constant-time / masked implementations are required for both ML-KEM and ML-DSA in production, not only in the HSM context.

3. Apple commits to Merkle Tree Certificates — institutional backing for MTCs is now complete

The Apple Root Program MTC policy (Sept 21, 2026, pre-window but in-scope for the W42 cycle because the Apple draft policy is targeted for end of October 2026) announced that:

  • A draft MTC policy will be published by the end of October 2026 in the apple/apple-root-program GitHub repository, followed by a public feedback survey.
  • MTC CA Operator applications will begin by late summer 2027.

The draft MTC policy requirements (per Apple's published statement):

  • Three cosignatures per certificate: 1. ML-DSA cosignature (minimum ML-DSA-44) from the issuing Operator 2. Classical cosignature (minimum ECDSA-P256-SHA256) from the issuing Operator 3. ML-DSA cosignature (minimum ML-DSA-44) from a different Operator acting as a mirror
  • All cosigner keys in HSMs validated to FIPS 140-3 Level 3 or Common Criteria EAL 4+
  • Operators register IANA Private Enterprise Numbers (PENs) to serve as the basis for Trust Anchor Identifiers
  • Certificate validity capped at seven days (same as Chrome's MTC validity window)
  • An annual audit
  • Operators must publish "far more non-private operational detail, including a record for every domain validation"

Apple's rationale for the three-cosignature + 7-day validity design is bandwidth: "Post-quantum certificate chains are roughly an order of magnitude larger than the classical chains they replace, because TLS handshakes typically contain five signatures and two public keys. Experiments with ML-DSA signatures in traditional X.509 hierarchies showed 'significant degradation of TLS handshakes in real-world deployments.'"

Apple's S/MIME path is the parallel track — traditional X.509 with composite algorithms combining ML-DSA with ECDSA or RSA, citing that S/MIME has "lower certificate issuance volume and less latency sensitivity than TLS", so the increased cert sizes are "less disruptive to the existing X.509 ecosystem." The policy amendment will allow signing traditional X.509 certificates with composite algorithms combining ML-DSA with ECDSA or RSA. ML-DSA provides security against quantum attacks; ECDSA/RSA provides decades of deployment experience.

7. ePrint cluster — lattice cryptanalysis continues to consolidate around (Concealed) ILWE

The week's ePrint cluster — papers that hit the IACR Cryptology ePrint Archive in or near the Oct 10 → Oct 17 window — confirms a two-track lattice-PQC research programme: deployment-protocol (W42: 2026/2480 WireGuard + ML-DSA + ML-KEM) and cryptanalytic surface (W42: 2026/2402 lattice blind-sig break; carry-over 2026/2091 ILWE soft-analytic).

ePrint 2026/2480 Post-Quantum Certificate-Based PSK Provisioning for WireGuard via Mutual TLS, DTLS, and QUIC: Design and Performance Study (Che Hang Ng/Emil Künstner/Bastian Küppers/Theodor Schnitzler/Misha Glazunov — likely academic + industry co-author mix) — presents an architecture that provides WireGuard's resistance against future quantum adversaries. The approach preserves the core protocol, minimizes deployment effort, and adds certificate-based identities to WireGuard peers. PQ resistance is achieved via an external mutually authenticated control channel built on the TLS 1.3 handshake and layered on top of TCP, UDP, or QUIC. Key establishment includes ML-KEM. An auxiliary shared secret is derived from the exported keying material of the resulting session and injected into WireGuard's native Pre-Shared Key (PSK) interface. The control channel is authenticated with X.509 certificates issued by a PKI using ML-DSA for PQ certificate signing. The paper develops a Tamarin model of the composition layer and symbolically analyzes the secrecy, binding, and provisioning-order properties of the interface between TLS 1.3 handshake and WireGuard, identifying the critical conditions necessary for secure deployment. The design is evaluated for handshake latency and CPU time for classical, pure PQ, and hybrid key establishment, classical and ML-DSA certificates, and the three transports, comparing it against an existing PQ WireGuard baseline.

For freedom-tech: this is the first formal Tamarin-verified hybrid PQ + ML-DSA-authenticated WireGuard deployment architecture — directly relevant to mesh networks, decentralized VPN overlays, and any freedom-tech system currently running WireGuard and considering PQ migration. The "external TLS 1.3 control channel + native PSK injection" pattern is reusable for other protocols that lack native PQ negotiation (OpenVPN, IPsec, custom RPC).

ePrint 2026/2402 Revisiting Lattice-based Blind Signatures Again (Yi-Fu Lai + Yu Yu — Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Oct 7) — shows an attack on a lattice-based blind signature proposed at Crypto'20 (the paper cites a 2020 paper but the specific scheme is not named in the abstract). The attack is under the honest key + honest-but-curious signer setting against the blindness notion by exploiting correlations between tree leaves. The paper also discusses how to repair the scheme.

For freedom-tech: blindness breaks of lattice-based blind signatures are rare and the specific weakness (correlations between tree leaves in a hash-function-based framework) is the same class of bug pattern that has hit lattice KEMs in prior work; freedom-tech implementers who use or plan to use lattice-based blind signatures (e.g., for anonymous credentials, e-cash, anonymous attestations, decentralized identity systems) should track this paper.

ePrint 2026/2091 Keep Track of Your Errors: Solving ILWE and Improving Attacks on ML-DSA (ElGhamrawy/Eisenbarth/Hermelink/Rabich/Sieck/Streit/Thietke/Zhang — U-Luebeck/DFKI/MPI-SP/Fraunhofer AISEC/TUM/RUB) — W41 carry-over, published Sept 28, but in scope for the W42 cycle — shows that the number of signatures required for ML-DSA side-channel secret-recovery attacks has been drastically overestimated — distribution hints (Eurocrypt 2025) can be derived from Integer LWE (ILWE) samples and a belief-propagation-based solver recovers the secret key efficiently. In a noise-free attack setting, the approach reduces the average number of signatures required by a factor of 62 — from 139 million to 2.25 million — compared with linear regression, with larger improvements under noise. The paper also analyzes a recently discovered timing leakage in ML-DSA and shows how to solve for the secret key with far fewer signatures.

For freedom-tech: the ML-DSA side-channel threshold for practical key recovery is now in the low millions, not hundreds of millions — comparable to the threshold for ECDSA side-channel key recovery. Implementers must enforce constant-time discipline on every signature operation, not only on the private-key generation step, and masked implementations are required for ML-DSA in production for high-value keys (e.g., firmware signing, code signing, root CA keys).

The Cloudflare ML-KEM measurement that the ePrint 2026/2091 paper cites as "as of August 2026, 59% of Cloudflare's requests use ML-KEM" is the production-deployment benchmark for the W42 cycle — confirms that ML-KEM is the dominant PQ KEM in production TLS traffic and that the ML-KEM side-channel surface is the most consequential PQC attack surface in deployment today.

ACM CCS 2026 LoTRS paper (carry-over)

LoTRS is the first construction in which a TRS variant is obtained by combining: (i) an aggregated signing layer — a two-round lattice-based multisignature protocol producing an aggregated signature relation, with (ii) a selection-hiding layer — a t-out-of-N proof that hides the chosen ring element supporting that relation. The concrete construction instantiates the aggregated signing layer using the lattice-based scheme from Crypto'23 and the selection-hiding layer from Esgin et al.'s lattice-based one-out-of-many proof (IEEE S&P'22). For freedom-tech: LoTRS is a practical lattice-based threshold ring signature — relevant to group signing for Nostr/Bluesky-style decentralized social networks, multi-signature Bitcoin/Ethereum wallets with ring-anonymity, and DAO governance with ring-anonymized voters.
