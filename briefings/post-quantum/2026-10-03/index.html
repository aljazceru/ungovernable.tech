  6. ePrint 2026/2113 (Sept 25, Branco/Döttling/Müller-Quade/Quan) — "Post-Quantum PKE and More from a Noisy Unstructured Linear Algebraic Assumption: Beyond LWE and LPN" is the most consequential cryptanalytic result of the week for freedom-tech. The paper introduces an NLA (noisy linear algebraic assumption) that is plausibly hard given an oracle that breaks even constant-noise-rate LPN (a strictly stronger LPN break than current lattice attacks provide), while remaining plausibly hard given an LWE oracle. The result: a single new natural assumption yields (i) public-key encryption, (ii) two-round maliciously secure oblivious transfer, and (iii) one-time circular-secure SKE. The relevance for freedom-tech: diversification beyond the lattice-only assumption of ML-KEM/ML-DSA — the paper is the first to make a single, structured assumption viable for all three primitives while explicitly being not LPN-broken. The paper's cryptanalysis rules out (1) combinatorial ISD-style attacks, (2) dimension reduction, (3) dual lattice attacks. Important boundary: this is a new hardness assumption; it is not a threat to ML-KEM, ML-DSA, or SLH-DSA; it is a diversification track.

For freedom-tech: the 2029 date is Google’s migration target, not a predicted date for decrypting recorded traffic; the unified framework gives a reduction-style argument for the hybrid composition (classical KEM + PQ KEM) that RFC 10024 implements operationally.

1. AWS-LC v5.11.0 (Oct 1) closes the September line and opens October

AWS-LC shipped v5.11.0 on 2026-10-01 23:32 UTC (release page, commit fa9bc8d, PR #3610). The release is the sixth AWS-LC release since Sept 1 (v5.5.0 through v5.10.0, plus v5.11.0 — seven total in the Sept 1 – Oct 1 window), maintaining the weekly cadence.

PQ-relevant changes in v5.11.0:

Notable: the ML-KEM BMI2+AVX2 gate is the most operationally consequential PQ change in the release. AWS-LC's ML-KEM C reference implementation is CAVP-validated; the AVX2 fast path is a performance optimization that has historically been gated on runtime detection of AVX2 support. The fix tightens that gate to also require BMI2, because the AVX2 implementation uses BMI2 instructions (specifically _pdep_u32 / _pext_u32) and would fail to link or produce undefined behavior on silicon that lacks BMI2. The fix is correct, but operators on legacy x86-64 hardware should verify the fallback path is being exercised as expected (no perf surprise). For embedded and IoT paths, this is a non-event (most embedded targets are ARMv8 and use aarch64 codepaths, not x86-64).

The fixed-point signing design decision, first publicly stated by Perlner in August 2025 and now embedded in the in-clearance draft, is the substantive change operators should be planning for. The single, precisely specified signing procedure (initially 32.32 for keygen, 64.64 for signing) that produces identical KAT outputs is what unblocks validation testing. For freedom-tech deployers: the FIPS 206 design constraint means a single signing-procedure implementation can be CAVP-validated, which is the prerequisite for inclusion in FIPS 140-3 modules. DigiCert's "wait until final" position holds — the IPD is necessary but not sufficient for production FN-DSA rollouts. The COSE draft draft-ietf-cose-falcon-04 (Prorock, expired 2026-09-16 per the IETF draft page) has not been re-uploaded as a -05 in this window, which is a maintenance concern; operators citing it should verify the document state on the datatracker before relying on the text. 4. ePrint 2026/2113 (Sept 25) — the diversification track beyond LWE/LPN

The Branco-Döttling-Müller-Quade-Quan paper "Post-Quantum PKE and More from a Noisy Unstructured Linear Algebraic Assumption: Beyond LWE and LPN" (ePrint 2026/2113) is the most consequential new cryptographic-assumption paper of the week for freedom-tech. The construction's key property: the NLA is plausibly hard against an oracle that breaks even constant-noise-rate LPN, while still being plausibly hard against an LWE oracle.

Why this matters for freedom-tech: the lattice-only assumption of ML-KEM and ML-DSA has been the single load-bearing assumption of the NIST PQC standardization. The Sept 25 note's Mattsson recommendation (ML-KEM + HQC hybrid for diversification) was the operational workaround — but HQC is also lattice-derived (code-based, with a lattice-closest reduction), so the diversification is partial. 2026/2113 opens a path to an NLA-derived PKE primitive that is provably not in the LPN-broken regime even if LWE weakens. For a deployer fielding PQC on the wire, the practical implication is: in 12-24 months, there will be additional PKE candidates worth evaluating that are structurally distinct from lattices. Track this paper for replication and implementation maturity.

Important boundary: 2026/2113 introduces a new hardness assumption; it does not break ML-KEM, ML-DSA, or SLH-DSA. The paper's contribution is in the assumption landscape, not in the primitive landscape. Deployers should not delay ML-KEM / ML-DSA / SLH-DSA adoption in anticipation of a 2026/2113-derived primitive — the standardization-and-deployment gap would be 3-5 years even on an accelerated path. The paper's value is in expanding the diversification menu for long-lived deployments that want non-lattice alternatives.

5. ePrint 2026/1992 (Sept 23) — Google 2029 deadline formalized

The Khurana et al. paper "Bridging the Cryptographic Transition: A Unified Framework for Post-Quantum Key Exchange" (ePrint 2026/1992) introduces a formal security framework for migrating classical KEX to PQ KEX, framed against the Google 2029 internal PQC migration deadline. The deadline is the central research framing: Google has established 2029 (deliberately ahead of the 2035 NIST/federal horizon) as the year for completing PQC migration in its production stack. The motivation, per the paper: faster-than-expected progress in (a) quantum computing hardware, (b) quantum error correction, and (c) revised resource estimates for quantum computers to break current cryptographic schemes.

For freedom-tech: the 2029 Google deadline is the first private-sector PQC deadline published by a hyperscaler. The NIST/federal horizon is 2030/2031/2035 (Phase 3/4/5 per OMB M-26-15); the 2030/2031 dates are for priority systems (HVAs, high-impact), not the full estate. The 2035 date is the backstop for the rest. Google’s 2029 target is a migration deadline. It cannot be used to infer a three-year interval between recording traffic and a future quantum decryption capability. The paper's unified framework gives a reduction-style argument that the hybrid composition (classical KEM + PQ KEM) implemented in RFC 10024 is security-preserving under the standard IND-CCA assumptions on both components. Cross-reference Hybrid KEM Migration.

For embedded / side-channel-exposed deployments, the q-randomization countermeasure (937 bytes firmware) restores ~4 bits of security loss instead of >140. Continue moving away from RSA for long-lived signing keys; hybrid Ed25519+ML-DSA-65 per Hybrid KEM Migration. - [ ] ML-KEM + HQC hybrid is the most explicitly-named diversification strategy on the standards track (per Mattsson's Sept 25 pqc-forum post). For operators with the freedom to deploy non-finalized primitives, this is the standards-track-aligned diversification path. HQC standardization is expected 2026-2027; operators can field HQC hybrid before the standard finalizes. Cross-reference HQC. - [ ] MIKE (isogeny-based, research-grade) can be fielded in operator-controlled settings where only isogeny-based options fit (per Obenour's Sept 25 forum post). Hybridize with ECDH and Kerberos-style symmetric keying so security holds if any component holds. - [ ] ePrint 2026/2113 (NLA beyond LWE/LPN): track for replication. Do not delay ML-KEM / ML-DSA / SLH-DSA adoption; treat 2026/2113 as a 12-24-month diversification track. - [ ] ePrint 2026/1992 (Google 2029): the 2029 date is a migration target, not a forecast of when recorded traffic becomes decryptable. The unified-framework result reinforces the hybrid-by-default operational recommendation.
