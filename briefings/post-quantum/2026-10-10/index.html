4. Pure PQ-TLS appeal — 16 days blocked, the longest appeal since 2025-Q4

The Sept 24 RFC Editor re-block on both draft-ietf-tls-mlkem-11 and draft-ietf-tls-mldsa-06 has now held for 16 days (and 29 days since mldsa-06 was last uploaded; 24 days for mlkem-11). The most recent substantive public record of the underlying dispute remains the freenode synthesis of the IETF's institutional response to Daniel J. Bernstein's repeated Last-Call objections against pure ML-KEM:

"the [draft-ietf-tls-mlkem] document remains an information document with a recommended status of 'N' in the IANA registry meaning that there was no consensus to recommend this group." — Sept 25 freenode synthesis (article)

The freenode piece documents the IETF's substantive response to three categories of objection:

  1. Compositional-security debate — Weyl/Soatok thread; the IETF's institutional position is that hybrid (not pure) ML-KEM is the operationally-endorsed default; the pure draft is "informational" rather than Standards-Track. The freenode piece cites the Soatok Dreamseeker July 2026 last-call post: "CNSA 2.0 style profiles and other FIPS-adjacent procurement rules do not always allow hybrid PQ ... Telecom and government buyers still ask for RFCs."
  2. NSA-conflict claims — Appelbaum-vs-NIST thread; the IETF's institutional position is that the public-record NSA involvement with the FIPS 203 authoring process is procurement-context disclosure, not "merit-of-the-specification" influence; the freenode piece cites Quynh of NIST's public claim that "the FIPS 203 authoring group had no NSA meetings and that NSA had zero authorship" challenged by Appelbaum against 9 July 2026 FOIA material citing "secret NSA and NIST PQC meetings, NSA weight on pqc@nist.gov, comments from 'Donna and the NSA' incorporated into a PQC NISTIR that FIPS 203 cites."
  3. Repeated-last-call procedural — Bernstein-and-collaborators thread; the IETF's institutional position is that WG Last Call has been called more than once under more than one draft number, all without a working-group-level consensus shift, and that the formal consensus call has been appropriately made under the RFC 9282 / RFC 2026 procedures.

No new public-facing communication from the IETF SecDir, IESG, or RFC Editor on the appeal has surfaced this week. The tls@ietf.org archive shows no new substantive debate thread opened in the 16-day window. The institutional processing of the appeal appears to be at the RFC Editor administrative-review stage (the AD and chairs are reviewing the substantive complaints; no new institutional action has been published).

For freedom-tech deployers: the operational default in 2026 remains hybrid X25519MLKEM768 (RFC 10024) — for both key exchange (draft-ietf-tls-ecdhe-mlkem, published as RFC 10024 in August 2026) and authentication (draft-ietf-lamps-pq-composite-sigs for PQ-or-composite X.509 certificate authentication). The pure PQ-TLS drafts are not blocking any production deployment: the ML-KEM-768 hybrid is supported by Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge, curl, nginx, AWS-LC, OpenSSL 3.5+, and Cloudflare's edge by default.

The IETF-127 TLS WG session (Nov 14 afternoon SF, 35 days out) is expected to discuss (a) the Sept 24 re-block, (b) any state changes between Oct 10 and Nov 14, (c) the path forward for pure PQ-TLS or a renewed push toward the hybrid-only operational default. 42 days to IETF-127. Cross-reference Hybrid KEM Migration.

9. IACR CHES 2026 hardware-PQC venue opens in 1 day

CHES 2026 (Cryptographic Hardware and Embedded Systems 2026) opens Oct 11, 2026 in Antalya, Turkey (Oct 11-15, 5-day workshop). CHES is the IACR's hardware-PQC primary venue — covering FPGA/ASIC acceleration of lattice-based KEMs and signatures, side-channel attacks on ML-KEM implementations, hardware-software co-design for PQ primitives.

For freedom-tech: CHES 2026 is the most-relevant pre-RWC-2027 venue for side-channel-resilience research on ML-KEM and ML-DSA implementations. The workshop's accepted papers are the most authoritative forward-looking signal of which side-channel mitigation techniques (masking, shuffling, constant-time NTT) have become production-credible. Operators fielding PQ on constrained devices (IoT, smartcards, TPMs, hardware wallets) should monitor the CHES 2026 program for production-credible side-channel mitigations.

The adjacent "FIPS 'n' Chips" Cryptographic Module Validation Conference (FNC2026) runs Oct 26-27, 2026 in Austin TX. FNC 2026 is the NIST CMVP (Cryptographic Module Validation Program) community's annual gathering — most-relevant for operators on FIPS 140-3 validated modules (AWS-LC FIPS 3.x, BoringCrypto FIPS, etc.).
