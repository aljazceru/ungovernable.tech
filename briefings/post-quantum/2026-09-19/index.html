TL;DR — This Week

  1. A clean negative on direct LWE-based AKE: ePrint 2026/2017, Ding/Yu/Pan/Cheng/Weng (Sep 14, ASIACRYPT 2026 accepted). The paper On the eCK Security of MQV-Style LWE-Based Authenticated Key Exchange definitively shows that ZZDSD-AKE (Zhang et al., EUROCRYPT 2015) cannot achieve eCK security — the 11-year open question is resolved negatively — and that GDLL-KE (Gao et al., IEEE TC 2018), the documented "hardened" variant with randomized noise, falls to a geometric signal-leakage attack in roughly 180 queries (vs ~1,700 for the unhardened ZZDSD). The attack is an implementation / signal-leakage class, not a cryptanalytic break of the underlying Module-LWE problem. The implication for the standards world is decisive: NIST's KEM-then-compose strategy (ML-KEM + signature) is vindicated; direct LWE-AKE is off the table as a research direction; the geometric signal-leakage framework becomes the new minimum bar any future direct LWE-AKE proposal must clear. The NIST-standardized primitives (ML-KEM, ML-DSA) remain secure at every parameter set. Cross-reference ML-KEM §implementation.

  2. The GSA 2026 PQC Summit convened September 16 in Washington DC (Department of Transportation West Atrium, hybrid Zoom for Government; GSA event, PQCA event). The agenda: federal migration strategy framework, PIV/identity in the PQ migration, AI-and-PQC threat intersection, crypto-agility patterns, GSA's contract-vehicle marketplace for quantum-readiness products. Vendor turnout confirmed (PQSecure, IBM, AWS, Cloudflare/infrequent on the gov side). No public release of new policy text observed in the 48 hours after the event; OMB M-26-15 Migration Plan submissions are still due October 22 and the federal migration timeline is unchanged. CNSA 2.0 first enforcement is now 104 days away (January 1, 2027), down from 110 at the September 12 update.

The ePrint 2026/2017 attack is on a different problem family (signal-leakage on MQV-style reconciliation) and does not bear on the lattice-primitive hardness. Cross-reference The Dihedral Coset Attack Line.

Freedom-tech implication: the ePrint 2026/2017 result is the strongest signal in this week's input that the KEM-then-compose architecture (ML-KEM + signature, as in RFC 10024 hybrid + RFC 9881 ML-DSA-65 X.509 + RFC 9980 OpenPGP composite) is the right design — direct LWE-AKE is off the table for standardization. The freedom-tech impact is minimal in the near term because the freedom-tech stack (TLS, SSH, OpenPGP, code signing, secure messaging, Monero/Bitcoin-style blockchain) was never planning to deploy ZZDSD-AKE or GDLL-KE. The impact is in the attestation of choice: the math now formally backs the architectural choice that was already operationally forced by NIST's standardization path. The remaining substantive moves (October M-26-15 plans, October Ethereum PQ Retreat, November IETF-127, December ASIACRYPT 2026) are all standards-tracking and procurement-tracking items, not primitive-revision items.

The Week in Detail

1. The eCK-Security negative — ePrint 2026/2017 (ASIACRYPT 2026 accepted)

The paper appeared at the IACR Cryptology ePrint Archive on 2026-09-14; it has been accepted for presentation at ASIACRYPT 2026 (Hong Kong, December 7–11). Authors: Ruoyu Ding (Jinan University), Tianrun Yu (Jinan), Yanbin Pan (Academy of Mathematics and Systems Science, CAS), Chi Cheng (Guangzhou University), Jian Weng (Guangzhou). Categories: Attacks and cryptanalysis. Keywords: Learning with errors, key exchange protocol, signal leakage attack, post-quantum cryptography.

The two targets:

  • ZZDSD-AKE (Zhang–Zhang–Ding–Sun–Dong, EUROCRYPT 2015): the MQV-style lattice analog of HMQV. Adapts MQV's static-plus-ephemeral key blending to ideal lattices. From 2015 onward, the central open question was: does it achieve eCK security (the LaMacchia–Lauter–Mityagin 2007 framework — adversary can compromise either the long-term static key or the ephemeral key material from a completed session, but not both from the same session)?
  • GDLL-KE (Gao–Dai–Lin–Li, IEEE TC 2018): documented as "robust against key-reuse" via a randomized-noise countermeasure.

The attack: a signal-leakage attack, not a Module-LWE cryptanalysis. The reconciliation step (called a "signal function") that converts the noisy LWE-based approximate agreement into an exact shared-secret bit is shown to leak enough information about the static secret that an adversary who can observe many sessions (under the active eCK adversary who gets the ephemeral key) can recover the static key.

The query counts:

Protocol Queries to recover static secret
ZZDSD-AKE ~1,700 (definitively fails eCK)
GDLL-KE ~180 (the "hardened" variant — its ad-hoc randomization does not stop the geometric attack)

Why this is not a break of ML-KEM: ML-KEM is a KEM (key encapsulation mechanism), not an AKE (authenticated key exchange) protocol. It does not have reconciliation-style signals that are reused across sessions in the same way. The attack class (signal-leakage on MQV-style reconciliation under active adversary with ephemeral-key compromise) does not apply to ML-KEM's IND-CCA2-secure Fujisaki–Okamoto transform construction. Cross-reference ML-KEM §implementation-hardening.

The broader message: the direct LWE-AKE research program (2012–2026) is now formally narrowed. The geometric signal-leakage framework is the new minimum bar. The KEM-then-compose architecture used by ML-KEM + signature (Ed25519, ML-DSA, or hybrid) is the architecturally defensible choice and is what NIST has standardized. The CRYPTO 2026 concurrent result showing that ML-KEM-based AKE achieves eCK-PFS security via a new authenticated forward-secure KEM construction points to one proven forward direction — itself composable with the KEM-then-compose model.

For a freedom-tech deployer: no action item. The result is epistemics — it confirms that the KEM-then-compose architectural choice was the right call, which is what the operational freedom-tech stack (TLS 1.3, SSH, Signal PQXDH, iMessage PQ3, OpenPGP RFC 9980, Bitcoin Taproot) already implements. There is no parameter-set revision to track; ML-KEM-768 / -1024 is unchanged.

3. The PQ-TLS publication pipeline — three documents, three stages

The IANA "OK - Actions Needed" qualifier on mlkem-10 is the substantive delta this week. It indicates IANA has confirmed the registry changes are needed and the IANA action itself is the only remaining step before the registry record is updated. That is procedurally one step closer to RFC publication — though the RFC Editor's own first-edit cycle still has to complete and an RFC number still has to be assigned.

The composite ML-DSA queue drag continues to be the IANA version-changed re-verify on the -19 vs the -18 that the queue first-edited on. The September 5 estimate of "4–10 weeks further" should now be read as "5–12 weeks further" given the queue has not been touched in 24 days and counting. Composite ML-DSA in X.509 PKI is the most-cited near-term publication, and the queue drag is not procedural dispute; it is RFC Editor bandwidth against IANA review back-cycle.

Composite ML-KEM (X.509 PKI) — separate document, separate trajectory. draft-ietf-lamps-pq-composite-kem-21 was posted 2026-09-01 and is in IESG Evaluation::Revised I-D Needed state — a downgrade relative to the composite-sigs document's queue position. This means substantive review feedback has come back from the IESG that requires a revised draft. The composite-KEM X.509 PKI stream is therefore behind the composite-sigs stream in publication prospects, not ahead. The September 12 note did not catch this; this is a correction.

4. The federal procurement clock

Unchanged from prior weeks; the GSA Summit was the on-ramp event, the M-26-15 deadline is the first deliverable, the January 1 enforcement is the first pass/fail gate. For a federal contractor or vendor, the operational checklist is:

  • HSM: FIPS 140-3 validated module supporting ML-KEM, ML-DSA, SLH-DSA in the IKE/TLS code paths the agency's contracts exercise. (FIPS 140-2 sunset Sep 21 → historical.)
  • TLS: hybrid X25519MLKEM768 (RFC 10024) as the wire default; ML-DSA-65 X.509 leaf (RFC 9881) when the issuing CA pipeline supports it.
  • SSH: mlkem768x25519 or hybrid (per the OpenSSH/curve25519 implementation).
  • Code signing: hybrid Ed25519+ML-DSA-65 (per the Bernstein June 2026 analysis; August 8 update §3) for high-value keys; SLH-DSA alone for conservative backup; FN-DSA / FIPS 206 when finalized (late 2026 / early 2027).
  • Encrypted archives (multi-decade): hybrid ML-KEM-1024 key wrap + AES-256-GCM.
  • Identity / PIV: PQ-capable path is in the M-26-15 plans due Oct 22; the September 16 Summit was the canonical venue for the PIV/identity track discussion.
