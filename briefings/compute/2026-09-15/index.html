Macro Thread — Phala's 78B Token Day: Z.ai's Stack Now Carries 70.5% of All Phala Throughput

The most important structural observation from this week's Phala reads: the supplier mix has narrowed onto Z.ai's stack in a way that no other DePIN inference network has matched, and the underlying token totals are now the largest measured on any DePIN inference surface.

Date Phala home-page daily tokens Active models Source

Sep 8 86.5B 10 phala.network home-page server-render
Sep 10 (24h window) 78.09B 10 (GLM-5.3 43.54% + GLM-5.3 Flash 26.96%) @PhalaNetwork X (status 2098088689067008476)
Sep 13 36.7B 10 phala.network home-page server-render

What the four-row table tells you:

  1. Phala cleared 78B tokens/day twice in seven days. The Sep 8 read of 86.5B (visible as the home-page 2026-09-08 timestamp) is the largest single-day server-render reading on record for any DePIN inference network. It is +113% above the Sep 2 reading of 40.4B flagged in the Sep 8 weekly, and +136% above the Sep 13 reading of 36.7B.
  2. The mix tells you why. GLM-5.3 (Z.ai flagship reasoning model) alone accounts for 43.54% of all Phala throughput on the Sep 10 day; GLM-5.3 Flash (Z.ai multimodal 320B MoE with 18B active params, Aug 27 launch on Phala) carries another 26.96%; together Z.ai's two-SKU stack represents 70.5% of all Phala daily tokens. The remaining 30% is split across DeepSeek V4 Flash, Kimi K3, Qwen3.6/3.8-27B, Muse Glimmer 30B, Gemma 4 31B, and others. This is the single tightest supplier concentration in DePIN inference and is structurally different from AkashML's flatter spread (which the Sep 8 weekly measured at 23.6B/week #6 on OpenRouter) or Aethir Mesh LLM (which is mostly B200/H200 token throughput, not OpenRouter's provider page).
  3. Why Z.ai's stack resonates with Phala specifically. GLM-5.3 (released Aug 14, 743B base model, post-training gains) is a long-horizon coding/agent model with a 1M-token context window; GLM-5.3 Flash is the multimodal 320B MoE variant with 18B active params. Phala's confidential-AI pitch is the privacy-preserving route for enterprise code agent and document analysis workloads — which is exactly the Z.ai value proposition. Z.ai's stated reasoning: developers want open-source SOTA capability + OpenAI-compatible routing + sealed private inference. Phala is the only DePIN network hitting all three.
  4. The pattern is also visible on Phala's models page. Sep 13 reads 12 distinct SKUs across Z.ai, Qwen, Meta, DeepSeek, MoonshotAI, NVIDIA, Google (Nemotron 3.5 Lightning at $0.08/M input is the price-floor SKU; Kimi K3 at $3.00/M input is the ceiling). The catalog is the deepest open-weight DePIN menu on OpenRouter by SKU count; AkashML is at 6.
  5. What is the steady-state? The four readings (40.4B, 86.5B, 78.09B, 36.7B) tell you Phala's home page captures a most-recent rolling peak and that intra-week variability is large. The conservative floor is now ~36-40B/day (vs ~28B in the Sep 1 weekly's reading); the rolling-peak ceiling is 86.5B. The Phala team's Sep 10 X post is the first time they have publicly claimed a specific 24h-token figure tied to a model-mix breakdown.

What the Phala Trajectory Means for the DePIN Inference Stack

Three observations:

  1. Phala is the only DePIN inference network with confirmed 70B+ daily-token days. AkashML tops out at ~24B/week (#6 on OpenRouter per Sep 1 weekly). Aethir Mesh LLM and io.net IO Intelligence route through separate surfaces. Gonka's measured daily volume (per crypto.news coverage) is much lower. Phala's Stripe/OpenRouter deal — closed Jul 18 per the Aug 4 weekly — is the gating event that explains the 28B → 86B ramp in 60 days.
  2. Z.ai as the upstream lab is a structural choice, not a market accident. Z.ai delayed the GLM-5.3 weights two weeks for safety testing (Axios Aug 14: "China's Z.ai holds GLM 5.3 release over hacking risks" — 84.5% CyberGym, 2,400 security flaws found to date). The model is the strongest open-weights reasoning model on public coding benchmarks (Terminal Bench 3.0: 4.6 → 28.3 vs GLM-5.2; DeepSWE 46.2 → 66.9; Agents' Last Exam CLI 23.8 → 28.5). The pitch is "best open weights + best open-source SOTA + best confidential inference" — and Phala owns the third leg exclusively.
  3. The audit overhang is now fully cleared. Phala's zkSecurity audit (announced Aug 13, first OS-level TEE platform audit) plus SOC 2 Type I, HIPAA, and ISO 27001-in-progress are the buy-side checklist. The Sep 8 weekly noted the structural critique ("audit was the open question") is now answered.

What the roadmap answers structurally:

  1. The "burn + variable fee" line item from the Sep 8 weekly now has four concrete operational forms. (a) Q1 2026's IDC v2 introduces "full on-chain transparency for service fees and rewards" — so the variable fee becomes a transparent on-chain parameter rather than an opaque Foundation discount. (b) Q2 2026's AI Workload Marketplace v2 + zk-proof validation prototype means the burn can be gated by zk-verified compute epochs (the natural counterpart of the Q2 zk-proof R&D). (c) H2 2026's CaaS pricing is the recurring-revenue surface where the burn acts as a deflating counterweight to enterprise ARR — making ATH's relationship to ARR explicit. (d) Aethir v3 late 2026 ships the multi-chain compute dashboard so ATH tracks utilization across deployments.
  2. The chain migration is the unannounced structural risk. The roadmap lists it as a 2026 commitment without naming the destination chain, the migration mechanics, or the holder-migration terms. Akash's analogous AEP-79 Shared Security decision (Dec 31 target) is the only parallel in DePIN compute right now. Two simultaneous 2026 chain decisions in two DePIN compute networks is a sector-defining moment.
  3. The Sep 7-14 blog cadence is unusually high — six pieces in seven days. Sep 7 Neocloud Debt and the GPU Collateral Value Problem; Sep 8 ACCELERATE Uses Demand-Led Capacity Planning; Sep 9 The Anthropic-Lambda Deal and the AI Compute Market; Sep 9 Aethir's 12-Month Strategic Roadmap (above); Sep 14 What NVIDIA's Hugging Face Acquisition Changes. This is the most aggressive Aethir content cadence in 2026 and reads as the structural answer to "ATH's value accrual low for no fee distribution and no buy-and-burn" critique that has defined the ATH bear case since the Coinbase Sep 3 perp delist.

Counter-Reading — CaaS Pricing is the Most Important Single Line in the Roadmap

The H2 2026 Compute-as-a-Service pricing model entry is structurally the most important line on the roadmap because:

  1. Recurring-revenue contracts paid through the Strategic Compute Reserve make ATH an institutional-grade asset class for the first time. Today, the SCR holds $344M of staked ATH; with CaaS in place, that stake backs enterprise GPU bookings on a multi-quarter cadence — the kind of demand floor that turns ATH from a DePIN utility token into a Treasury-style reserve asset.
  2. Hybrid cloud partnerships ("Oracle, Tencent, AWS") are the most aggressive distribution statement Aethir has ever made. The Sep 8 weekly noted Aethir was publishing positioning copy against Stripe/OpenRouter. The roadmap's "partner with major clouds" line is the explicit answer: Aethir is positioning to sell to enterprise as a GPU commodity (through Oracle, Tencent, AWS) rather than only to crypto-native clients. The implication for Akash, io.net, and Render is that the competitive surface for enterprise compute now includes legacy cloud channels.
  3. Aethir v3 + AI orchestration APIs in late 2026 ship the multi-chain compute dashboard. That is the moment Aethir becomes directly comparable to AkashML (which has been selling through OpenRouter for 8 months at #6). Whether Aethir ends up inside OpenRouter's catalog or only sells direct is a Q3-Q4 2026 decision to watch.

Three corrections from the Sep 8 weekly:

  1. AEP-67 has been delivered since Jul 17 — not "Last Call since May 2025." The Sep 8 weekly's claim was based on the akash.network/roadmap/aep-67 page formatting that still says "Last Call"; the canonical AEP/ROADMAP.md at the repo gives 2026-07-17 as the completion date. This is consistent with the Sep 1 weekly's confirmation that AEP-67 was delivered alongside AEP-46 on Aug 30/31; the dates in the repo are the source of truth.
  2. AEP-46 is delivered (Aug 30) — not "Draft since Dec 2024." The AEP's spot-instance design is now in production.
  3. AEP-85 (Buildpacks-Powered Deployments) is a new entry for Sep 30 that was not in the Sep 8 weekly. Buildpacks is the Heroku-style automatic buildpack orchestration that lets developers push source code without writing Dockerfiles; combined with AEP-36 (Custom Domain Nov 15) and AEP-11 (Managed Services Market Dec 15), the Q3-Q4 2026 Akash roadmap is now a three-pillar developer-experience buildout: buildpacks (Sep 30), custom domains (Nov 15), managed databases (Dec 15).

What this means for Akash's competitive position:

  1. The Sep 30 AEP-85 deadline is the next concrete checkpoint. Buildpacks is the gateway from "developer with Dockerfile" to "developer with git repo" — the lowest-friction developer path in DePIN compute. Phala already ships phala deploy -c docker-compose.yml; Akash matching that with Buildpacks is the structural race for "easiest DePIN inference platform to deploy to."
  2. AEP-79 Shared Security Dec 31 is the Cosmos-to-Solana decision point. The AEP is the formal home for the migration debate; if it ships by Dec 31 with a "stay on Cosmos" stance, that's a binding network decision. If it ships with a Solana migration plan, Aethir's parallel chain-migration becomes the second high-profile 2026 migration in DePIN compute.
  3. The competitive surface against Phala. Phala leads on confidential AI inference (10+ models on OpenRouter, 86.5B/day peak); Akash leads on general-purpose Kubernetes workloads and is about to ship the developer-experience gap closer (AEP-85 Buildpacks). The two networks are complementary rather than competitive on most workloads, but the enterprise developer audience that wants private LLM inference + standard k8s manifests + domain-named endpoints + managed databases has a real path through both.
