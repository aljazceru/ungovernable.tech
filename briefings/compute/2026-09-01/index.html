Phala — dstack Goes Cross-Cloud; Phala Network OpenRouter Catalog Hits 24B Tokens/Week

Cross-cloud dstack: AWS Nitro, GCP TDX, Phala native, with one measurement stack

Phala's dstack whitepaper (Aug 2026) and the One Confidential Compute Framework post document the same workload definition now packaging into three runtimes:

Developer: docker-compose + signed manifest
                │
                ▼
   ┌────────────────────────────┐
   │ dstack (workload identity, │
   │ KMS, gateway, attestation) │
   └────────────┬───────────────┘
                │
   ┌────────────┼──────────────┐
   ▼            ▼              ▼
Phala TDX   GCP TDX+TPM    AWS Nitro
(native)    (EIF/CCV)      (Enclave)
                │              │
                └──────┬───────┘
                       ▼
               Per-app keys
               (released only after
                measurement-verified
                attestation passes)

Three architectural moves make this non-trivial:

  • Portable confidential containers: workload portability across TDX / SEV-SNP / Nitro without rewriting the compose file. The "last-mile" packaging step (EIF construction on Nitro, CCV image build on GCP) is owned by the platform adapter, not the developer.
  • dstack-KMS on-chain: keys are bound to a measurement-derived app identity, not a cloud account. Forward/backward secrecy with key rotation; multi-party computation supported for distributed trust.
  • Governance collusion protection: dstack's policy smart contracts require multisig (default 2/3) and a timelock delay before code swaps can release new keys — operators cannot silently swap binaries or exfiltrate secrets.

The threat model is the cloud operator, not the silicon vendor. dstack's position is that the silicon (Intel TDX, AMD SEV-SNP, NVIDIA Hopper/Blackwell CC, AWS Nitro) is the necessary root of trust, but policy enforcement and identity binding must live above it. This matches the OYM (Aug) reading of the TEE landscape.

Phala now serves 21 models on OpenRouter, zero-retention, US-jurisdiction, with a 24B-token week ending Aug 31. The active model mix (per official @PhalaNetwork breakdowns):

The honest counter-signal: PHA at ~$0.022, 99% off its 2024 high, while billed tokens have grown ~7x in three weeks. Token value-capture from confidential-inference volume remains the unsolved design problem in the sector.

Akash — AEP-46/67 Deliveries; 23.6B OpenRouter Tokens/Week; Homenode Update

AEP-46 (Spot/Preemptible Instances) and AEP-67 (Console Bid Screening) land

Both roadmap items due Aug 30 (AEP-46) and Aug 31 (AEP-67) cleared the deadline window:

  • AEP-46 — Spot Instances / Reserved Instances: providers can now whitelist tenant wallets (reserved) and offer discounted interruptible capacity (preemptible). Two-sided enterprise feature set — addresses the largest objections from AWS/Google Cloud workloads about bid predictability and lease longevity.
  • AEP-67 — Console Bid Screening: Console pre-screens providers against a deployment's requirements, so users see only providers who would actually bid. Replaces the abandoned live per-provider inventory query design. Zero-bid-UX is a direct response to the Console friction cited in Aug 4.

The AEP-85 (Buildpacks-Powered Deployments, Sept 30) is next — replaces the broken Build & Deploy flow (hard-coded JS frameworks, broken for Python/Go/Rust/.NET) with Cloud Native Buildpacks. That lands before the Oct 3–5 Akash Annual Conference + Hackathon in SF — a likely venue for L1/shared-security RFP (AEP-79, targets Dec 31) news.

OpenRouter position: 23.6B tokens/week, 489.1B lifetime

AkashML is now the #2 decentralized provider on OpenRouter (behind Phala) at 23.6B tokens/week — making AkashML and Phala the only DePIN networks in the OpenRouter top 10 by recent throughput. AkashML's narrower catalog (6 models) but heavier per-model volume shows the "Kubernetes-of-inference at AWS-replacement prices" thesis is now landing measurable demand, not just capacity.

Homenode and the GPU supply gap

The Homenode Beta (consumer/prosumer GPUs) covered in previous weekly remains in testing. The supply-side gap — Messari's 58 providers avg, network low in Q1 — is the binding constraint on Akash growth, and Homenode is the team's experimental answer. Akash Insiders are stress-testing provider onboarding, hardware compatibility, and inference performance ahead of the alpha release. Watch the October conference for beta-to-alpha graduation.

Bacalhau — NCL Protocol Now Default; v1.7.0 Enterprise Hardening Lands

Bacalhau, the Compute-over-Data orchestration framework for IPFS/Filecoin/S3/edge, shipped a meaningful transport upgrade in the last fortnight:

  • PR #4734 (merged): NCL (NATS Client Library) Protocol — async messaging between orchestrator and compute nodes, with separate control and data planes, ordered delivery, heartbeats, checkpoint-based recovery. Replaces the request/response bprotocol as the default in v1.6 (NCL opt-in via BACALHAU_PREFER_NCL_PROTOCOL=true); bprotocol remains the stable default for backward compat.
  • PR #4687 (NCL integration tests): compute nodes now advertise their supported protocols in NodeInfo, orchestrator negotiates the preferred protocol, Buildkite runs both transport types in CI.
  • Health/heartbeat config: HeartbeatInterval 15s, HeartbeatMissFactor 5, CheckpointInterval 30s, ReconnectInterval 10s base → 5m max.

The v1.7.0 release notes (covered Aug 25 but worth restating for the new transport): S3 partitioning (object / date / regex / substring distribution, automatic per-partition retries), Docker-in-Docker (DinD) compute-node image variant, and enterprise support options. Bacalhau's positioning — bring compute to data rather than move data to compute — keeps it the strongest choice for data-local analytics and regulated workloads where egress is the binding constraint.

Sources: Bacalhau v1.7.0 release notes, PR #4734 NCL Protocol, PR #4687 NCL async, bacalhau.org.

Oyster's market position is the TEE-coprocessor layer for other networks — Verida is one customer, Bonfida's decentralized SNS gateway is another, and the OYM TEE comparison and Verifiable Compute's Hardware Problem essays are the sector's open-reference threat-model docs. The x402 stack is now the cleanest monetization layer in confidential compute.

Freedom-Tech Takeaways & Recommendations

  1. The OpenRouter provider page is now the DePIN inference storefront. Phala and AkashML are top-6 listed providers by last-week tokens. Treat the listing, model count, weekly token bar, and "Zero retention" badge as primary marketing surfaces — not blog posts. For users, the actionable sovereignty response is to confirm direct-API contracts and verify the underlying provider's chain-attestation path; the routing-layer capture is now real.

  2. dstack going cross-cloud changes the confidential-compute conversation. The same workload definition now packages into Phala native, GCP TDX+TPM, and AWS Nitro EIF runtimes with one measurement-derived identity and one KMS path. The next architectural question for any team building on TEEs is no longer "which silicon" but "do I want vendor-portability (dstack), single-cloud depth (Phala native only), or a non-TEE alternative entirely." Per the OYM TEE comparison and Verifiable Compute's Hardware Problem — the silicon is the necessary root of trust, but the policy and identity layer above it is the operational question.

  3. Grayscale's amended S-1 is a procedural engagement signal, not a verdict. The Aug 31 amendment keeps TAO's spot-ETF decision inside the originally-forecasted window. Plan for three scenarios: approval (institutionalizes the DeAI basket), rejection (TAO retest toward ~$164, Covenant-era decentralization question re-opened), or extension. The 43% Grayscale AI-fund TAO allocation is the manager's vote of conviction through the uncertainty.

  4. Exploit Summit is the sector's single most consequential gathering in the next four weeks. New City Gas, Montreal, Sept 28–29. dTAO underwriting under ETF context, post-Covenant validator-competition restoration progress, subnet consolidation, and the inaugural 100-subnet-teams showcase. If you can only attend one DePIN event in 2026's second half, this is the one — every subnet team, validator, and institutional observer will be in the room.

  5. Gensyn's verification surface needs a workload. Delphi is generating fees; RL Swarm (the original credibility pitch) is paused. The 100-day retrospective crystallizes a real risk: the most rigorous parts of Gensyn's stack (Verde, Judge) have no production workload to secure right now. Until RL Swarm resumes, the buy-and-burn economics live on Delphi's fee flow alone — meaningful, but not what the original thesis sold. Watch for any RL Swarm resumption signal before the April 2027 cliff.

  6. Aethir's IDC tokenomics update is the template other DePINs may copy. Burn-mechanism + variable platform fee explicitly tied to on-chain revenue is the cleanest design statement in the sector. Watch Aethir's ATH action in the 30 days after the policy ships — if it works (revenue accrues, price stabilizes), Phala (PHA at ATL with 24B tokens/wk), io.net (IDE burns), and others have a ready-made answer to the "usage up, token down" paradox. If it doesn't, the design space narrows.

  7. Gonka's governance rhythm is the credibility signal. Proposal 96 (SPRT small-sample stabilization, Aug 21) is small but well-documented. The pattern — surgical, transparent, host-action-clear proposals under audit pressure — is consistent with a team shipping the Sprint rewrite carefully. Still pre-production stability, still OTC-only, but the engineering discipline is visible.
