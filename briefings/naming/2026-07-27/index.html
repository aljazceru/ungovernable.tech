Decentralized DNS Monday — July 27 2026 Weekly Update

TL;DR — This Week

  • Handshake 2nd Halving (~July 22-23, 2026): Block reward halved (2000 → 1000 HNS). ViaBTC pool closure and Namebase shutdown earlier in June set the stage; community sees opportunity for further decentralization. Price impact minimal/short-term as expected.
  • ENS Governance & Adoption Momentum: DAO activates 8-member Security Council with veto power (July 21, 2026) to block malicious proposals. Turkish Directorate of Communications highlights cbiletisim.eth for official dWebsite/comms. Q2 eth.limo report emphasizes ENS as foundational decentralized web layer. Discussions around "Verified by ENS" for project launch constitutions, anti-rug proofs, and AI/agent identity.
  • PKARR / pkdns / Pubky: Continued maturation with no major breaking changes. Iroh integration stable; public pkdns.net resolvers gaining adoption. Focus on production readiness and SDKs (Rust/JS). Low public hype but strong technical progress in sovereign P2P naming.
  • Other Projects: Nomen, Cardano DNS/DNNS, GNS remain in low-activity/experimental phases. No significant new deployments or papers surfaced in the past two weeks.
  • Freedom-tech takeaway: Hybrid strategies win — use ENS for discoverable, governable public identities with real-world pilots; PKARR for censorship-resistant pseudonymous/self-sovereign names in off-grid/mesh/Nostr stacks; monitor post-halving HNS for sustainable blockchain root alternatives. Run your own resolvers (pkdns, GNS) for maximum sovereignty.

The Week in Detail

1. Handshake (HNS) — Post-Halving Landscape

The second halving occurred around July 22-23, 2026 (block ~340,000 range). Block reward dropped from 2000 HNS to 1000 HNS. This follows the June 1 ViaBTC pool closure (~50% hashrate) and June 2 Namebase full shutdown (dominant registrar/marketplace wound down, coins/names returned to users).

  • Impact & Community Sentiment: Short-term price pressure expected but viewed positively for decentralization. Reduced centralization of mining and marketplace functions. Next halving ~2029.
  • Relevance: HNS remains a strong candidate for replacing ICANN root in a blockchain-anchored model, though adoption lags ENS. Post-halving supply dynamics may support long-term holders/miners.
  • GitHub / Resources: https://github.com/handshake-org (core repos); https://www.namebase.io/ (now defunct marketplace, historical data).

Relevance to Ungovernable.tech: Strong for uncensorable TLDs. Combine with Mesh & Off-Grid Naming for hybrid solutions. Monitor miner decentralization metrics post-halving.

2. ENS (Ethereum Name Service) — Maturing Identity & Governance Layer

ENS continues to lead in visibility and real-world traction.

  • Security Council Activation (July 21, 2026): ENS DAO approved an 8-member council with veto power over malicious proposals. Major governance hardening following recent DAO attack concerns.
  • Real-World Adoption — Turkey: Republic of Türkiye Directorate of Communications officially using cbiletisim.eth for decentralized communications and AI/blockchain initiatives (confirmed/highlighted in late July updates). One of the first government bodies to adopt on-chain identity.
  • Verifiable Commitments & "Verified by ENS": Community proposals for projects to publish signed "launch constitutions" (supply schedules, treasury, multisig rules, etc.) under their .eth name on IPFS. Positions ENS as immutable timestamped proof of promises — powerful anti-rug and trust tool.
  • Decentralized Web & Identity Expansion: eth.limo Q2 2026 report frames ENS beyond wallet names into dWebsites, social graphs, gateways, and AI/agent verification. Discussions on ENS-powered decentralized social networks.
  • Technical: Continued L1 focus (Namechain abandoned). Gas costs remain low; v2 tooling (roles, permissions) from May maturing.
  • Links: https://ens.domains/ ; ENS Blog (ens.domains/blog); Twitter discussions on verifiable constitutions and dWebsites.

Relevance to Ungovernable.tech: Excellent for public-facing, discoverable names with growing institutional credibility. Use for project identity, official comms, and verifiable claims. See ENS for deeper technical details. Strong complement to Nostr WoT.

3. PKARR / pkdns / Pubky Ecosystem — Sovereign P2P Naming

Quiet but steady progress in the most censorship-resistant naming approach (DHT + public keys, no blockchain dependency).

  • Integrations & Production Use: Iroh (n0-computer) continues leveraging PKARR for endpoint discovery alongside other mechanisms. Pubky Core advancing semantic social graphs and homeserver discovery. pkdns production DNS bridge stable with public instances (pkdns.net).
  • Recent Activity: No major releases in the immediate past two weeks, but crates (pkarr v6.x) and SDKs remain actively maintained. Focus on reliability, caching, and easy self-hosting.
  • Freedom-tech strength: Records live on Mainline DHT (millions of nodes). Ideal for pseudonymous identity, mesh networks, Reticulum, Nostr, and fully offline-capable naming. Long cache TTLs provide resilience.
  • Links: https://github.com/pubky/pkarr ; https://github.com/pubky/pkdns ; https://pubky.org/ ; Awesome-pubky: https://github.com/aljazceru/awesome-pubky .

Relevance to Ungovernable.tech: The closest realization of "your keypair = your name." Prioritize for Mesh & Off-Grid Naming, off-grid networks, and privacy-maximalist setups. Run local pkdns resolver for full control. See PKARR and Mesh & Off-Grid Naming.

4. Other Projects & Research

  • Nomen, Cardano DNS, DNNS, GNS: Low visible activity. Cardano Catalyst proposals for cross-chain bridging with Handshake continue in background. Nomen (Bitcoin + Nostr) remains experimental.
  • Academic/Other: No major new papers surfaced. Broader blockchain DNS surveys from earlier in 2026 remain relevant.
  • Overlay Naming: Tor .onion and I2P continue as mature alternatives for specific use cases (see Overlay Naming - Tor .onion and I2P).

Freedom-Tech Takeaways & Recommendations

  1. Layered Approach Recommended: Use ENS for human-readable, widely resolvable public identities and institutional pilots. Use PKARR for self-sovereign, seizure-proof names in P2P, mesh, and privacy-focused applications. Handshake as a potential long-term root replacement if post-halving economics sustain it.
  2. Self-Host Where Possible: Run pkdns or GNS resolvers locally/system-wide. Avoid reliance on single public instances.
  3. Monitor Governance: ENS Security Council is a positive hardening step; watch DAO proposals. HNS post-halving miner distribution important for true decentralization.
  4. Real-World Signals: Government adoption (Turkey) and verifiable commitment proposals show decentralized naming moving from crypto-niche to practical infrastructure.
  5. Next Steps for Research: Track HNS hashrate distribution post-halving; follow ENS "launch constitution" implementations; test pkdns in mesh scenarios with Reticulum or Iroh.
