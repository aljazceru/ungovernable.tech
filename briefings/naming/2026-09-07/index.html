2. ENS DAO governance Sep 1 burst — five threads, one newsletter, one report

The ENS DAO forum's "latest" page for Sep 1 is unusually dense — five new threads + the bi-weekly newsletter + voting bulletin. Catalogued:

(a) ENS DAO Newsletter #119 — 09/01/2026 (Newsletter, 23 views, 0 replies). Bi-weekly on Tuesdays at 11 AM ET. The release of Newsletter #119 sits exactly between the audit-competition window (Aug 18 → Sep 14) and the Term 7 voting period — a normal cadence beat, not an emergency.

(b) Report: Measuring the Impact of ENS's P-256 Precompile Migration (Ecosystem Discussion, 89 views, 1 reply, Sep 1). The headline data: 1.12B gas saved across all eligible DNS imports over the realized period Mar 9 → Jul 27 2026 — equal to 82.3% of what those imports would otherwise have cost. RIP-7212 fallback contract shaves P-256 verify from ~330k gas (Solidity fallback) to ~3.4k gas (precompile) — about a 100× reduction. The precompile was active on Ethereum L1 and key L2s by mid-2025; ENS DNSSEC imports are the biggest beneficiaries because P-256 ECDSA is what most DNSSEC chains actually use. For PKARR/DNSSEC-style key-rooted naming, this is the gas-cost argument for P-256-based identity (WebAuthn / passkeys / Secure Enclave) over secp256k1 — the same trade-off avalanche's ACP-204 makes.

(c) ENSIP-28: ENS Name Owned Accounts (Standards, 88 views, 11 replies, Sep 2). First seen as a Standards thread, now seeing discussion. Continues the ENSv2 contract refactor conversation — "name owned accounts" suggests a clean EOA-like interface for ENS names themselves, decoupling the holding account from the resolution process.

(d) [RFC] Privacy-Preserving Names: ENSIP for Stealth Address Resolution] (Standards, 236 views, 7 replies, Sep 1). The most cryptographically novel ENSIP in the batch. Stealth addresses per-address per-recipient unlinkable payment targets — a privacy primitive most associated with Monero and, recently, Ethereum (ERC-5564 / ERC-6538). An ENSIP for stealth-address resolution means a .eth name can resolve to a fresh per-transaction stealth address without leaking the recipient's primary wallet. This is the first time stealth addresses are reaching the naming layer. The relevance to PIR and the broader censorship-resistance stack is direct: a recipient's wallet becomes ephemeral on-chain, with the ENS name as the only stable identifier.

(e) ENSIP: Text Record Attestations (Standards, 25 views, 0 replies, Sep 1). A standard for cryptographic attestations attached to text records — likely signing pubkeys, capability attestations, or third-party claims. Connects to ENSIP-16 (DNSSEC), ENSIP-17 (optimistic record challenges), and the broader CCIP-Read verifier surface.

(f) Voting Period Bulletin — Term 7 (Meta-Governance voting, 91 views, 3 replies, Sep 1). Working group stewards Term 7 election cycle.

(g) [Executable] SPP3 Marketplace RFP Award: Nomentum Labs (Grails)] (Active Proposal, 94 views, 2 replies, Sep 1). SPP3 (Service Provider Program round 3) grant: Nomentum Labs / Grails for marketplace work. Continues the Blockful / JustaName service-provider trajectory.

(h) "SafeNotes Shutting Down — DAO-Wide" (24 views, 0 replies, Sep 1). ENS DAO infrastructure decommissioning note — the SafeNotes internal documentation/wiki tool being sunset is a routine operational cleanup.

Cross-reference: ENS; Overview - Decentralized DNS §"ENSv2"; Aug 31 weekly §"ENS Forum thread"; Aug 10 weekly §"ENS Next Era".

3. ICANN Board LA workshop + Reveal Day — announcement expected this week

The ICANN Board met Sep 4–6 in Los Angeles (per ICANN Board Chair Tripti Sinha's preview blog post), with the New gTLD Program 2026 Round on the agenda. The Aug 13 ICANN announcement stated Reveal Day's specific date would be announced in mid-September 2026, with publication no later than nine weeks after Aug 12 (≈ Oct 13).

CircleID's post-close analysis ("After the Close: What the 2026 gTLD Round Has Already Revealed", Kevin Murphy, ≈ early Sep) gives the post-cleanup numbers:

  • ~1,300 net applications (≈300 below 2012 round's ~1,930)
  • ~1,100 replacement strings filed (low-cost collision protection — applicants protect their primary strings by also filing variants at lower cost)
  • The "more than 1,600" ICANN figure includes both primary and replacement-string applications; the net after admin check is ~1,300
  • Reveal Day publication expected for October specifically (Lexology and CaptainDNS analysis agree with the ICANN ≤ Oct 13 hard deadline)

What to watch this week: ICANN announcement mid-Sep window is now open. The Reveal Day date is likely to drop between Sep 7 (post-Board-meeting) and Sep 14 (one week later). The ICANN Board meeting minutes + Chair blog follow-up are the earliest signal; the formal Reveal Day date announcement is the binding event.

Freedom-tech stakes: Reveal Day publishes the public portions of all applications passing Administrative Check — primary strings, variant strings, replacement strings, contention groups. The diff against Unstoppable Domains' 19+ application portfolio, ENS's .ens application, and Handshake's on-chain TLDs is the audit task. Any string that gets delegated to a competing applicant creates resolution ambiguity for existing Web3 name holders in every ICANN-resolving browser.

Key-rooted schemes are structurally immune to this collision pressure — PKARR, Tor .onion, GNS, Yggdrasil, cjdns all use a public key or petname as the root identifier and don't compete for ICANN string space.

Cross-reference: Unstoppable Domains (UD's 19+ applications); ENS (.ens gTLD application); Handshake (on-chain TLDs); Aug 31 weekly §"Reveal Day"; Aug 24 weekly §"ICANN 2026 round closure".

7. DNS-AID / stealth ENSIP — quiet week, but adjacent ENSIP signals a privacy turn

DNS-AID draft-02 (Mozley/Williams/Sarikaya/Schott/Damick; May 27 2026) expires Nov 28, 2026. No new draft has been posted this week. The Cloudflare "Agent Readiness" DNS-AID checker enforcement-of-draft-01 issue flagged in the Aug 24 weekly is still unaddressed.

Adjacent signal: the ENSIP Stealth Address Resolution RFC (Sep 1) is a parallel track that touches the same "agent-to-agent identity discovery" problem space DNS-AID addresses, but at the naming layer rather than the DNS record layer. Both efforts converge on the same long-term question: when agents are communicating, how does one agent discover the canonical identity of another without leaking metadata? DNS-AID's answer is _agents.<domain> TXT records with DNSSEC validation. The ENSIP Stealth Address Resolution answer is ENS name → ephemeral stealth address → unlinkable payment target. The two approaches are complementary — DNS-AID for capability advertisement, stealth ENS for transaction-level unlinkability.

A third related IETF draft is draft-nemethi-aid-agent-identity-discovery-00 (Balazs Nemethi, last updated Mar 16 2026) — a non-DNS agent identity scheme. Status: I-D Exists, no stream defined. Not actively shipping.

Cross-reference: Aug 24 weekly §"DNS-AID"; Aug 3 weekly §"DNS-AID — naming layer for the agentic web"; Identity (Nostr/Pubky stealth-address-equivalent features).

8. GNUnet / GNS — 0.29.0 holds, no point release

GNUnet 0.29.0 (released Aug 20 2026 per the official news page and Wikipedia infobox) remains the latest. No 0.29.1 point release this week. The Aug 20 release notes call out:

  • Major version → protocol break with 0.28.x. All peers must upgrade in lockstep.
  • Transport subsystem regressions addressed.
  • General stability fixes.

The GNS protocol surface is unchanged at the spec level — the protocol break is in transport. GNS lookup-privacy story is unchanged: queries against the GNS DHT are encrypted and unlinkable to the queried name. The strongest of the decentralized-naming schemes for lookup-privacy, but the smallest deployed base (single-digit thousand active nodes per rough community estimates).

Freedom-tech takeaways

  1. The ENS audit closes Sep 14, not Sep 22. Researchers holding valid findings still have T-7 days to submit via the Immunefi portal. Daemon operators should verify any ENS Labs contact claiming audit-related work references the canonical Immunefi competition page (immunefi.com/audit-competition/audit-competition-ens) and the 0x39befB5b7F778278Cc1d9Ee3a4F1e06CBA163293 vault address.
  2. ENSv2 mainnet is now on a mechanical clock. Sep 14 close → Sep 20 fix → Sep 28 freeze → Sep 29 dispute → Oct 26 publish → mainnet deploy. If the Oct 26 results publish with no Critical, ENSv2 contracts go to mainnet inside Q4. The window between Oct 26 and Q4-end is the mainnet deploy window.
  3. ICANN Reveal Day announcement is this-week expected. The Sep 4–6 Board LA workshop was the staging event; the ICANN-specific announcement should land between Sep 7 and Sep 14. When it does, the diff against UD's portfolio + ENS's .ens + Handshake's on-chain TLDs is the audit task.
  4. Stealth-address ENSIP is a signal that privacy-by-default is now in the naming layer. The Sep 1 RFC for Stealth Address Resolution is the first time the stealth-address primitive (Monero / ERC-5564) is being elevated to an ENS standard. For Daemon identity work, this opens the door to ENS-fronted unlinkable payment targets — a recipient's .eth name resolves to a fresh stealth address per transaction, breaking chain analytics without breaking the human-meaningful identifier.
  5. P-256 precompile data is the gas argument for WebAuthn/Passkey-native identity. 1.12B gas saved across ENS DNS imports → P-256 is the right curve for DNSSEC-anchored identity on Ethereum. PKARR's Ed25519 choice is still correct (no DNSSEC use case), but any future ENSIP for hardware-key-backed identity should use P-256 not secp256k1.
  6. Pubky release plateau is a stabilization phase, not abandonment. 19 days without a tagged release from a project that ships ~2-3 crates per month is normal. The next release wave is most likely late September, after the ENSv2 audit closes.
  7. Oct 11 KSK rollover is the operational deadline. T-34 days. Daemon operators must verify KSK-2024 (Key Tag 38696) in trust-anchor set before Oct 11 via delv @resolver . DNSKEY against IANA trust anchors. Self-sovereign infrastructure (pkdns, GNS-to-DNS proxies, unbound/knot/BIND 9.11+ resolvers) is the failure surface.
  8. Handshake's hns-rs 0.3.1 cohort is the only shipping line. PR #952 is a long-running conversation thread; the Rust protocol stack is the operational reality. Until a new hsd or hns-node-rs mainnet canary appears, the HNS "uncensorable DNS-on-chain" story is at v0.3.1 protocol-stack maturity.
